According to [[@2023margolis]], **domain specificity**—roughly, the matter of *being directed at* a particular domain or subject matter—has three factors:
- **Distinctive input:** Domain specificity can be determined by distinguishing which inputs “turn on” a cognitive mechanism from other information that can be accessed.
- **Specialized internal processes:** Determining domain specificity based on distinctive processes accounts for mechanisms that take diverse input, but are nonetheless directed at a particular **content domain** by virtue of the mechanism’s tuning to processing that content.
- **Distinctive output:** Outputs in the same **conceptual cluster** straightforwardly show domain specificity in cases where inputs and internal algorithms are unknown.

Further, [[@2023margolis]] argue that the presence of any one of these factors in a cognitive operation suffices to determine domain specificity.

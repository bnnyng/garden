# Overview

Researchers in [[@2019lehmanSuprising]] define **digital evolution** as “evolutionary processes in which the algorithm of evolution and the genetic material evolved is instantiated digitally.” The general principle behind this field is that the three necessary and sufficient [[Three abstract conditions for evolution, after Dennett|conditions for evolution]] do not depend on a biological substrate (see: [[Biological vs. digital evolution]]).

[[@2019lehmanSuprising]] identify four ways in which digital evolution experiments can lead to “surprising” results: misspecified fitness functions, unintended debugging, exceeding experimenter expectations, and convergence with biology.

# Historical examples

- [Tierra (computer simulation)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_(computer_simulation)>) (1992)
	- Artificial life system, early example of evolution by natural selection in a digital medium.
	- Characterized in [[@2019lehmanSuprising]] as a “surprising” example of **convergence with biology** via **evolution of parasitism**.
- Sims, Karl (1994). “[[@1994sims|Evolving 3D Morphology and Behavior by Competition]].”
- Cheney et al. (2013). “[[@2013cheney|Unshackling Evolution: Evolving Soft Robots with Multiple Materials and a Powerful Generative Encoding]].”
	- Extension of Sims’ work to the **morphology** of soft robots.
	- Example of **unintended debugging** by evolving virtual creatures that exploited **imperfectly simulated physics**: few-celled creatures would go through the floor of the simulated environment, and the simulator would supply them with “free energy” to correct the glitch, which the creatures used as a form of locomotion.
	- Example of **convergence with biology** via evolution of locomotion similar to that with complementary (opposing) muscle groups and bones.

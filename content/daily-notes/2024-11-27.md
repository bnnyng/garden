		- Humans exceptional in the capacity for distant friendships.
		- [[@2017mercier]] – reasoning make possible communication and coordination in the absence of social bonds—why?
	- Language as a cognitive signpost → why did we develop it to be this way → bring up social reasoning hypothesis → 
		- Where to stick the argument that the only time we need to rationalize is in response to something related to other people? Read Buckner on inferences
			- Deductively valid inferences are just coincidence
			- People think better when they reason out loud $\implies$ people are not reasoning this way intuitively
		- An LLM does not have an internal locus or world model to switch
- How to tie this in with ideas about deductive reasoning not really being an innate part of thought? What else could there be left?
	- Associationism, direct inference from sensory perceptions
	- What is the format of mental representations? Maps
		- Goal is to always make shortcuts that update a world model
		- World model is what permits one to make certain predictions/inferences—but how? Via “dreaming”? Putting together laws we know about the world in new ways?
		- What do I do when I have a “gestalt switch” in math? I know what needs to be done, but not exactly how to get there?
- Reasoning places new things into our perception/makes us perceive things differently, but not necessarily?
	- “Things” include non-physical
	- So what determines perception? Back to affordances—a “perception” or a perspective is simply the space of possibilities/connections one can make to the object in perception. These tend to align with “reasons” and “causal explanations” simply because it is more adaptive for them to do so—making decisions unmoored from the physical world would probably not lead to much survival.
	- Lions making a gestalt switch, similar to mathematicians—ultimately want to move beyond the space of articulating reasons; the sort of switching is not different between humans and animals? The example from Buckner—*how* did the lions learn that giraffes, in this given circumstance, could *afford* food? 
	- Great mathematicians are not simply the ones who are good at reasoning
- Feels like if I just think hard enough about a thing, I can come to know why I understand things
	- Not really a circumstance of my reasoning where I do not pretend I am articulating it to someone else
	- If we do not take immediate action, then it is simply to articulate the reason. Otherwise we are making a probabilistic inference
	- Certainty is an attitude about probabilities still. We have a space of possible affordances and we have probabilities over them. How do we decide between things of different probabilities?
- Social basis of reasoning $\implies$ what is the role of reasoning? To make new things a part of our perception, i.e., automatic response to objects in the world. 
	- Mainly to place things in the perception of others, so that they don’t have to trust our word.

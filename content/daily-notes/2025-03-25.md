#### [[MATH-GU4053|Algebraic Topology]]

- Previously: Exact sequences, chain complexes
	- [[(Theorem) A short exact sequence of chain complexes induces a long exact sequence of homology groups]] (“on homology”)
- Application: $(X, A)$ pair of spaces, $i : A \to X$ inclusion, then $0 \to C_p(A) \to_{i_*} C_p(X) \to C_p(X, A) \to 0$.
	- **Relative chains:** $C_p(X, A) = C_p(X)/\textup{Im}(i_*)$, applying boundary operator?
	- This induces a LES of homology
		- $H_p(A) \to_{i_*} H_p(X) \to H_p(X, A)$ ($H_p(X, A)$ is the **relative homology**, homology of $X$ relative to $A$, defined by $H_p(C_*(X, A))$; $H_p(X)$ is smth called **absolute**
		- $H_{p-1}(A) \to _{i_*} H_{p-1}(X) \to H_{p-1}(X, A)$ and so on, repeating with index one smaller… 
	- Variant $B \subset A \subset A$
		- SES of chain complexes: $0 \to C_p(A,B) \to C_p(X, B) \to C_p(X, A) \to 0$
		- LES of triple: $H_p(A, B) \to H_p(X, B) \to H_p(X, A)$
	- Exactness means at each spot, image of map coming in = kernel of map going out

**Relative homology** ([[Homology of general chain complexes]])

> “Every time you see a thing defined on objects you should ask what it does on morphisms.”

- Relative homology has maps induced by $f: (X, A) \to (Y, B)$ where $f: X \to Y$ continuous w/ $f(A) \subseteq B$.
	- Induced: $f_* : H_p(X, A) \to H_p(Y, B)$
- Special case: can always talk about relative homology of $X$ relative to empty set. Set $A = \varnothing \subseteq X$.
	- Chains on empty set is just 0 group at all degrees, so isomorphic i.e., $C_p(X) \to_\cong C_p(X, \varnothing)$
	- **Upshot:** prove anything about relative homology also gives you smth about absolute homology

**Homotopy invariance of the induced map on relative homology**

- Theorem: the map on relative homology induced by $f: (X, A) \to (Y, B)$ is an isomorphism given the assumptions $f: X \to_\simeq Y$ is an h.e. and $f|A : A \to_\simeq B$ is too
	- [[(Theorem) (Relative) homology is homotopy invariant]]
	- Today: calculating homology of disk relative to sphere

**Excision theorem**

- Proof next time
- Set-up: subspace of a subspace $Z \subset A \subset X$, excise the smaller one
	- Inclusions $X \backslash Z \to_i X$ and $A \backslash Z \to_i A$, can regard this as a map of pairs with inclusions rel to the two different inclusions
	- Induces $i_*: H_p(X \backslash Z, A \backslash Z) \to H_p(X, A)$
	- Theorem: this is an isomorphism if closure $\overline Z$ in $X$ is in the interior of $A$.
- [[(Theorem) The relative homology of a subset is isomorphic to the subset with excision when the closure of the excision is in the interior of the subset]]

**Application of homotopy invariance and excision**

- [[(Theorem) The homology of the n-disk relative to its boundary is n for the pth group and 0 otherwise]]
	- Surprising that it is so small, and even more surprising that its not actually zero?
	- Finitely generated and nonzero
	- “Philosophical justification that singular homology is a good thing”
- Proof: induct on n—no calculations
	- Use h.e. of boundary with one point removed and R^n as the smaller subset, find LES corresponding to $\partial D^n \backslash \{ S\} \subseteq \partial D^n \subseteq D^n$
	- Show that everything has to be zero map due to the fact that h.e., then use homotopy invariance to show the zeroes
	- Then use excision to talk about the n-1 disk, $\{N \} \subseteq \partial D^n \backslash \{ S\} \subseteq \partial D^n$ where $N, S$ are north, south poles; follows that inclusion induces an isomorphism
	- Bdry with point removed isomorphic to $\mathbb R^{n-1}$, which is h.e. for $D^{n-1}$ and so have same relative homology, which we have presumably proved
- **Upshot:** $H_p(D^n, \partial D^n) \cong H_{p-1}(D^{n-1}, \partial D^{n-1})$
	- The most important, first non-trivial calculation of singular homology?

**Homology group of $n$-sphere**

- can be deduced from LES of pair
- Reconfirms that for $S^n$ higher, path-connected, $H_p(S^n)$ is $\mathbb Z$
	- For $S^0$ it is $\mathbb Z^2$
- Corollary: $S^n \simeq S^m \iff n = m$
	- Contradiction for $2, 3$-spheres: $H_2$ is different since $H_2(S^2) = \mathbb Z$ and $H_2(S^3) = 0$
- Corollary: $\mathbb R^n \cong \mathbb R^m \implies n =m$
	- Gives a recipe also for extracting the dimension: the degree for which $H_p$ is infinite cyclic
- Remark
	- Continuous surjection from $\mathbb R \to \mathbb R^2$
	- Also exist bijections $\mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R^m$ ([[Continuum hypothesis|continuum hypothesis]]?)

**A version of no retract?**

- Theorem: there does not exist a continuous $r : D^n \to \partial D^n$
- Shows usefulness of functors
- “Not super important that isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$, just that it is nontrivial”

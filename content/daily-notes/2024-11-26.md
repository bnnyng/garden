Recall: If $f: X \to Y$ a homeomorphism, then $f_*:\pi_1(X,x_0) \to \pi_1(X, f(x_0))$ is an isomorphism for all $x_0 \in X$. A lot of weaker functions also satisfy this!

>[!abstract] Lemma:
>Let $h, k: X \to Y$ be [[Homotopies|homotopic]]. Fix $x_0 \in X$, and set $y_0 = h(x_0)$ and $y_1 = k(x_0)$. Then there exists a path $\alpha$ in $Y$ from $y_0$ to $y_1$ such that 
>$$
>\hat \alpha \circ h_* = k_*,
>$$
>where $h_*, k_*$ are the induced functions on homotopy classes in $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ and $\pi_1(Y, y_0)$, respectively.
>#wip commutative diagram!
>Further, $h_*$ is injective/surjective iff $k_*$ is injective/surjective.

*Proof from [[MATH-GU4051|Topology]].*

Let $H : X \times I \to X$ be a homotopy from $h$ to $k$. Set $\alpha(t) = H(x_0, t)$, and note that $\alpha(0) = h(x_0) = y_0$. Claim: $\alpha$ is the desired path.

To show this, we define several auxiliary paths on the space $X \times I$. First, let $f$ be a loop based at $x_0$, and define loops at “top” and “bottom” by $f_0(t) = (f(t), 0)$ and $f_1(t) = (f(t), 1)$, making a sort of cylinder. Let $c$ be a path from the base point of $f_0$ to the base point of $f_1$, which goes directly up the side of the cylinder. We have
$$
H \circ f_0(t) = H(f(t), 0) = h(f(t))
\quad \quad
H \circ f_1 = k \circ f_1
$$
$$
H \circ c(t) = H(x_0, t) = \alpha(t).
$$

Now consider the four “edge paths” on the rectangle $I \times I$ defined by
$$
\beta_0(t) = (t, 0) \quad \quad \beta_1(t) = (t, 1)
$$
$$
\gamma_0(t) = (0, t) \quad \quad \gamma_1(t) = (1, t).
$$
Notice that we have a path-homotopy $\gamma_0 * \beta_1* \simeq_p \beta_0 * \gamma_1$; these are the two possible paths from the bottom left corner to the top right. Define $F : I \times I \to X \times I$ by $F(s, t) = (f(s), t)$. #wip 


>[!abstract] Theorem:
>If $f: X \to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence, then the induced map $f_* : \pi_1(X, x_0) \to \pi_1(Y, f(x_0))$ is an [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|isomorphism]].

- Upshot: $h, k$ are related by an isomorphism, but doesn’t make sense to say they’re equal

Let $P = \{a = x_0, \ldots, x_n = b\}$ be any partition of $[a,b]$. By the mean value theorem, in every interval $[x_{k-1}, x_k]$, there exists some $x_{k-1} < t_k < x_{k}$ such that
$$
f'(t_k) = \frac{f(x_k) - f(x_{k-1})}{x_k - x_{k-1}} \implies 
f(x_k) - f(x_{k-1}) = (x_k - x_{k-1})f'(t_k).
$$
Defining $t_k$ similarly for all intervals in $P$, we substitute into Equation \ref{EQ: Exercise 6, eq 1} to get that
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal L(f) 
    &= \sup_P\sum_{k=1}^n \sqrt{(x_k - x_{k-1})^2 + (x_k-x_{k-1})^2f'(t_k)^2} \\
    &= \sup_P \sum_{k=1}^n (x_k - x_{k-1}) \sqrt{1 + f'(t_k)^2}.
\end{align*}
Then as $n \to \infty$, we have $(x_k - x_{k-1}) \to 0$, and thus 
$$
\lim_{n \to \infty}\sqrt {1 + f'(t_k)^2} 
= \inf_{t \in [x_{k-1}, x_k]} \sqrt {1 + f'(t)^2} 
= \sup_{t \in [x_{k-1}, x_k]} \sqrt {1 + f'(t)^2},
$$
since continuity of $f$ implies that the image of each interval $f([x_{k-1}, x_{k}])$ approaches a single point and we know at least $t_k \in [x_{k-1}, x_{k}]$.

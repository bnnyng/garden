- [[Compactness]]
	- To prove something as a consequence of compactness, take a cover of balls with very small radius, and then taking finite subcover
	- **Equivalent statements of compactness:** [[(Theorem) Compactness, limit point compactness, and sequential compactness are equivalent on metric spaces]]
		- Further, if $X$ is in Euclidean space, then we have another **equivalence**: [[(Theorem) Heine-Borel]]
	- **Important theorems about compactness:** 
		- Compact $\implies$ closed and bounded
		- Closed subset of a compact space is compact
		- **Cantor intersection:** $A_n$ compact for all $n$, also countable nested collection $A_{n+1} \subseteq A_n$, then their intersection is nonempty (implied useful)
		- [[(Theorem) Bolzano-Weierstrass]]
		- [[(Theorem) Arzela-Ascoli]] I: sequence of function $X \to \mathbb R$, $X$ compact, sequence pointwise bdded and uniform equicontinuous $\implies$ $f_n$ has convergent subsequence
			- Homework 7 diff-eq equation; ODE existence (Rudin 25)
		- $A \subseteq C(X, \mathbb R$ subset of continuous function space on a compact space $X$. Then $A$ compact iff closed, bdded, unif equicontinuous.

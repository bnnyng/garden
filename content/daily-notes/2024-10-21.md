- Ways to show that a series diverges
	- Show the associated sequence does not converge to 0
	- If non-negative, show each element in the sequence is bounded below by a sequence which has a divergent series
- Canonical examples of convergent/divergent series for comparisons
	- Geometric: $\sum x^n$ converges when $x <1$, diverges when $x \geq 1$
	- P-series: $\sum 1/n^p$ converges when $p >1$ and diverges for $p \leq 1$ (notice that $p>1$ means $1/n^p \to 0$ also).  The harmonic series is the special case of $p=1$.
- Examples of convergent/divergent sequences
	- $n^{1/n}$ converges to limit $1$ always
	- In general, the $n$th root of some real $p$ converges to $1$ if $p >0$ positive

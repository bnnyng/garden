>[!example] Definition: Disjoint union
>Given a collection of sets $\{ X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$, their **disjoint union**, or **coproduct**, is 
>$$
>\coprod_{\alpha \in J} X _\alpha = \{ (x, \alpha) \ | \ \alpha \in J, \ x \in X_{\alpha}\}.
>$$
>For a single set $X$, while the usual union of $X$ with itself is simply the original set $X \cup X = X$, while $X \sqcup X$ involves two distinct copies of $X$ ❓.


- Products have projection maps, disjoint unions have injections $i_\alpha(x) = (x, \alpha)$
	- “Universal set that receives maps from each $\alpha$”
	- Universal property: $\coprod X$ is the universal set receiving maps from every X_alpha
	- Reversing all the arrows in the commutative diagram for disjoint union/coproduct (co = flip all your arrows around) gives the story for the [[Product spaces|product topology]]
- Back to [[Local compactness and compactification]], [[(Theorem) One-point compactification]]
	- Remember: closed subset of compact is compact, compact subset of Hausdorff is closed
	- $p$ is isolated = both open and closed
	- $\hat X = X \sqcup \{ p\}$ as a topological space (not interesting to add a point trivially to a compact $X$)
- $X$ not compact (e.g. $X = \mathbb R$) means $p$ is limit point, so $\overline X = \hat X$. *When $X$ is not compact, specifically called one-point compactification of $X$*.
	- $\overline X = \hat X$ usually a property of compactification – closure is the whole space
- Quotient spaces make “gluing process” precise (can take a closed disk, do a gluing process, and get something homeomorphic to 2-sphere)
	- $\mathbb R$ glued to make $S^1$
	- Box $[0,1]^2$ glued to make cylinder/annulus/$S^1 \times [0, 1]$.
	- Glue the circles from above together to make a torus, kind of $S^1 \times S^1$ but that’s vague…

#### [[MATH-GU4051|Topology]] lecture notes

>[!example] Definition: Separation axioms
>Let $X$ be a $T_1$ [[Topological spaces and open sets|topological space]], meaning singletons are closed.
>- $X$ is **regular** if for all $x \in X$ and any closed $A \subseteq X$ not containing $x$, there exist disjoint open sets $U, V \subseteq X$ such that $x \in U$ and $A \subseteq V$.
>- $X$ is **normal** if for all disjoint, [[Closed sets and closures|closed]] sets $A, B \subseteq Z$, there exist disjoint open sets $U, V \subseteq X$ such that $A \subseteq U$ and $B \subseteq Y$.
>
>It is clear that normal $\implies$ regular $\implies$ [[Hausdorff spaces|Hausdorff]] $\implies$ $T_1$.


>[!abstract] Theorems: Separation axioms
>1. [[Compactness|Compact]] and [[Hausdorff spaces|Hausdorff]] $\implies$ normal.
>2. [[Local compactness and compactification|Locally compact]] and Hausdorff $\implies$ regular.
>3. [[Metrics, metric spaces, and the metric topology|Metrizable]] $\implies$ normal.
>4. Regular and [[Countability axioms|second-countable]] $\implies$ normal.

Which spaces are metrizable?

>[!abstract] Theorem: Urysohn’s Metrization Theorem
>If $X$ is regular and [[Countability axioms|second-countable]], then $X$ is [[Metrics, metric spaces, and the metric topology|metrizable]].

Idea: Embed $X$ in a metric space, e.g., [[The countably infinite product of the real line with itself]] with the uniform or product topology.

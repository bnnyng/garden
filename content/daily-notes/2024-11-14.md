#### [[MATH-GU4051|Topology]] homework

```
    For clarity, we will denote the equivalence classes by $[x]_1 \in S^n/(x \sim -x)$ for $x \in S^n$ under $\sim$ and $[y]_2 \in \mathbb {RP}^n$ for $y \in \mathbb R^{n+1} \backslash \{ 0\}$. 
    
	
	% so it induces a continuous function $\bar f : S^n/(x \sim -x) \to \mathbb {RP}^n$ such that $f = \bar f  \circ q$ and $f(x) = \bar f([x]_1)$ for all $x \in S^n$. 
    
    % We see that $\bar f$ is surjective since given $[y]_2 \in \mathbb {RP}^n$, there exist $y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb R^{n+1} \backslash \{ 0\}$ such that $y_1 = y / |y |$ and $ y_2 = -y / |y|$, so $x_1 = i^{-1}(y_1)$ and $x_2 = i^{-1}(y_2)$ are in $S^n$ and satisfy $x_1 = -x_2$. Thus, there exists $[x]_1 \in S^n/(x \sim -x)$ such that $\bar f([x]_1) = f(x_1) = f(x_2) = [y]_2$. On the other hand, if $f([x]_1) = f([x']_1)$, then for any $t \in \mathbb R$ we can find some $i(x), i(x') \in \mathbb R ^{n+1} \backslash \{0\}$ such that $x = tx'$, so choosing $t=-1$ we have $[x]_1 = [x']_1$ and $\bar f$ is injective. 
    % https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/1032094/to-show-that-sn-v-sim-v-is-homeomorphic-to-mathbf-rpn
```

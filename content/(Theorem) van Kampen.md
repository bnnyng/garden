# Overview

The **van Kampen theorem** is used to compute the [[Fundamental groups|fundamental group]] of a space $X$ that is the union of some open $A, B$ with [[(Path-)connectedness|path-connected]] intersection. The first “special case” shows that given a point in the intersection $x_0 \in A \cap B$, the group $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|generated by]] the images of the two groups $\pi_1(A, x_0)$ and $\pi_1(B, x_0)$ under the [[Fundamental groups|induced homomorphisms between fundamental groups]] $(i_j)_*$ induced by [[Projection and inclusion maps|inclusion]] mappings. The van Kampen theorem states that $\pi_1(X, x_0)$ is actually *completely determined* by the two latter groups, the group $\pi_1(A \cap B, x_0)$, and the various homomorphisms between fundamental groups induced by inclusion.

# A special case

Recall the special case of the van Kampen theorem used to prove [[(Theorem) The fundamental group of the n-sphere is trivial in higher dimensions]]:

![[(Theorem) The fundamental group of the n-sphere is trivial in higher dimensions#^9db065]]

This can be rephrased in terms of [[Free products of groups|free products]] as follows:

>[!abstract] Lemma: Homomorphism out of the fundamental group of the free product of path-connected spaces is a surjection
>Suppose $X = \bigcup_{a \in J} A_\alpha$, where each $A_\alpha$ is open and [[(Path-)connectedness|path-connected]] and the intersections $A_\alpha \cap A_\beta$ are also path-connected. Let $x_0 \in X$ be any point. For each $\alpha \in J$, there exists an [[Projection and inclusion maps|inclusion]] $j_\alpha : A_\alpha \to X$ which induces a [[Fundamental groups|homomorphism between fundamental groups]]
>$$
>(j_\alpha)_* : \pi_1(A_\alpha, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0).
>$$
>Setting 
>$$
>\varphi_\alpha = (j_\alpha)_*
>\quad \quad
>G_\alpha = \pi_1(A_\alpha, x_0)
>\quad \quad
>G = \pi_1(X, x_0)
>$$ 
>the unique homomorphism $\varphi : * _{\alpha \in J} G_\alpha \to G$ out of the [[Free products of groups|free product]] is a *surjection*. Equivalently, the images of all $(j_\alpha)_*$ [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|generate]] $\pi_1(X, x_0)$.
>
>![[Pasted image 20241214223422.png|400]]

The homomorphism $\varphi$ just [[Path concatenation|concatenates]] loops, and the previous lemma showed that any $[f] \in \pi_1(X, x_0)$ is of the form
$$
[f] = [f_1] * \cdots * [f_k],
$$
where $[f_i] \in \pi_1(A_{\alpha_i}, x_0)$.

In particular, $\varphi$ works by applying either $(j_1)_*$ or $(j_2)_*$ to some element in the free product, depending on which fundamental group the element originally comes from.

- Corollary: if decomposition both simply connected, intersection path connected, then whole space is simply connected

# Weak van Kampen

>[!abstract] Theorem: Weak van Kampen
>If $X = A_1 \cup A_2$, each $A_i$ is open and [[(Path-)connectedness|path-connected]], $A_1 \cap A_2$ is [[Simply connected spaces|simply connected]], and $x_0 \in A_1 \cap A_2$, then the homomorphism
>$$
>\varphi : \pi_1(A_1, x_0) * \pi_1(A_2, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)
>$$
>is an [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|isomorphism]].

# Stronger van Kampen

>[!abstract] Theorem: Strong van Kampen (statement about the kernel)
>Let $X = U \cup V$ where $U, V$ are open and [[(Path-)connectedness|path-connected]], $U \cap V$ is path-connected, and $x_0 \in U \cap V$. Then the unique [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|homomorphism]] out of the [[Free products of groups|free product]]
>$$
>\varphi : \pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0) \to \pi_1(X, x_0)
>$$
>has kernel
>$$
>\ker\varphi = \langle \langle \{ (i_1)_*^{-1}(g)(i_2)_*(g) \ | \ g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) \} \rangle \rangle,
>$$
>where $(i_1)_* : \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) \to \pi_1(U, x_0)$ and $(i_2)_* : \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) \to \pi_1(V, x_0)$ are homomorphisms induced by inclusion mappings for the intersection.
>
>![[Pasted image 20241214223442.png|400]]

Equivalently, the kernel is the smallest [[Normal subgroups and quotient groups|normal subgroup]] containing elements of the form given above. This means precisely that the kernel has all elements $g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$, plus their *conjugations* by elements of $U_1, U_2$.

# Special cases

#### Intersection of open subsets is simply connected

The weak van Kampen, which requires $U \cap V$ be [[Simply connected spaces|simply connected]], is then a special case of the more general van Kampen theorem where $\varphi$ is a isomorphism since its kernel is trivial: if $g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0)$, then by definition $g \in 0$, so $\ker \varphi = \{ e\}$ is generated by the identity only. Since $\varphi$ is a surjection, it follows from [[(Theorem) First isomorphism theorem]] that 
$$
\text{im} \varphi  = \pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0) / \ker \varphi = \pi_1(U, x_0) * \pi_1(V, x_0).
$$ 
#### One of the open subsets is simply connected

Suppose $U$ is [[Simply connected spaces|simply connected]]. Then $\ker \varphi$ is generated by the set
$$
\{ (i_2)_*(g) \ | \ g \in \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0\} = \{ \text{image of }(i_2)_*: \pi_1(U \cap V, x_0) \to \pi_1(V, x_0)\},
$$
 as $(i_1)_*^{-1}(g) \in \pi_1(U, x_0) = \{ e \}$ is simply the identity element, and we have precisely
 $$
 \pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \frac{\pi_1(V, x_0)}{\langle \langle (i_2)_*(\pi_1(V, x_0))\rangle \rangle}.
 $$

# Examples and computations

A general strategy for constructing open sets $U, V$ that satisfy the hypotheses of van Kampen is to take some sets you think they “should be,” then make them to be a little bigger to be open and cover the space $X$. For example, when $X = S^1 \vee S^1$, we can take $U, V$ to be copies of $S^1$ with a small open neighborhood about the point that they meet at. Similarly, if $X$ is some surface constructed by “gluing” edges to make a polygon $P$ (e.g., the torus $S^1 \times S^1$ constructed from the square, or the genus-2 torus $\sum_2$ constructed from the octagon), one can take $U = P \backslash \{ p \}$ with some point removed and $V$ to be a neighborhood about $p \in P$. 

#### Simply connected intersection: The figure 8

Given $X = S^1 \vee S^1$, recall that the $\vee$ operation denotes the two copies of $S^1$ that intersect at a single point. Take $A_i$ to be one copy of $S^1$ together with a neighborhood of $x_0$ (this looks like a circle with an arc stuck to it). We know $\pi_1(A_i) \cong \mathbb Z$ for $i = 1, 2$, since each $A_i$ is [[Homotopy equivalence|homotopy equivalent]] to $S^1$ ($A_i$ [[Deformation retractions|deformation retracts]] onto $S^1$) and the fundamental group is invariant under homotopy equivalence. Further $A_1 \cap A_2$ (this looks like a curved cross) deformation retracts onto the single point $x_0$, so they have isomorphic fundamental groups
$$
\pi_1(A_1 \cap A_2, x_0) \cong \pi_1(x_0, x_0) = 0
$$
and $A_1 \cap A_2$ is [[Simply connected spaces|simply connected]]. Then the **weak van Kampen** implies $\pi_1(X, x_0) \cong \mathbb Z * \mathbb Z = F_2$, the [[Free products of groups|free product]] of rank 2.

By induction, the [[Fundamental groups|fundamental group]] of $n$ circles meeting at a single point is isomorphic to $F_n$, the free product of rank $n$.

#### One simply connected subspace: $S^1 \times S^1$

The [[Torus|torus]] $T = S^1 \times S^1$, considered as a quotient of the square, can be decomposed into the following: let $A_1$ be the filled square with a point removed, and let $A_2$ be a basic open neighborhood of that point. Then we have the following:
- $A_1$ has a [[Deformation retractions|deformation retraction]] to the boundary of the square, which has two pairs of sides $a, b$ with the same orientation. This is homeomorphic to $S^1 \vee S^1$, so $\pi_1(A_1) = \mathbb Z * \mathbb Z$ and is generated by $a, b$.
- $A_2$ is [[Simply connected spaces|simply connected]], so $\pi_1(A_2) = 0$.
- $A_1 \cap A_2$ is homeomorphic to the annulus, which can be [[Deformation retractions|retracted]] to $S^1$ and therefore has $\pi_1(A_1 \cap A_2) \cong \mathbb Z$, which is generated by a single loop $\omega$ about the circle.

The [[Induced homomorphism between fundamental groups|induced homomorphism]] $\pi_1(A_1 \cap A_2) \to \pi_1(A_1)$ maps $\omega \mapsto aba^{-1}b^{-1}$; informally, the loop $\omega$ is embedded as a loop around the hole in $A_1$, which is homotopic to the **commutator** of the generators $a, b$. Thus, van Kampen implies
$$
\pi_1(T) \cong \frac{\mathbb Z * \mathbb Z}{\langle \langle aba^{-1}b^{-1} \rangle \rangle} \cong \langle a, b \ | \ aba^{-1}b^{-1} =1 \rangle
$$
The generators $a, b$ commute, so this is precisely the presentation of the [[Abelian groups|abelian]] group $\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z$.

[[@2023barron]] argue that the most important factor for comparing computational architectures—and determining transitions between them—is what *resources* are available for changing **control flow**, or how parts of the architecture coordinate to transform and learn from information moving through the brain.

| Computational architecture type | Description of control flow                                                                                                                                                        | Implications                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Example systems                                              |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Decentralized                   | In single-celled systems, information moves through diffusion or active chemical transport; in multicellular systems, local interactions between sensory elements and “effectors.” | Capable of basic forms of **learning** (sensitization, habituation, quasi-associative). Structures exist to support evolution of a **nervous system**.                                                                                                                                                | - Hydra (*Hydra vulgaris*)<br>- Jellyfish <br>- Sea anemones |
| Centralized                     | **Feedforward sensorimotor transformation** controlled by a  distinguished **central nervous system**, which acts as a unified decision-making system.                             | ”Enables both **global coordination** and **local specialization**”, which make **complex, differentiated bodies** possible. New forms of learning (multi-modal, cross-modal recall, generalization and classification of information, context learning, latent inhibition, blocking and patterning). | - Flatworm (*Dugesia japonica*)                              |
| Recurrent                       | Output of a process can influence and control the operation of earlier processes.                                                                                                  | Representations can **reverberate** to remain active and influential in the network over time. Learning of **abstract relationships**. Basic **forward modeling** of decision-making consequences (i.e., planning).                                                                                   | - Insects                                                    |
| Laminated                       | Multiple parallel but interacting recurrent subsystems, allowing multiple operations to be performed on the same information at the same time.                                     | High-dimensional representations of information, including across multiple timescales and **multiplexing** (i.e., using the same representation for different purposes). Learning based on **practice**. High **evolvability** for new cortical functions.                                            | - Vertebrates                                                |
| Reflective                      | Can be modified by representations of itself, such that the informational architecture is specified **virtually** (e.g., via cognitive programs) rather than biologically.         | Efficient use of **symbolic languages**, enabling new information compression methods and hence new programs.                                                                                                                                                                                         | - Humans                                                     |

*One of many [[Definitions of mathematics]].*

Comparisons between mathematics and artists arose in the 19th century with the active construction of new [[Mathematical structure|mathematical structures]] (e.g., non-Euclidean geometries). For G. H. Hardy, mathematics is worth pursuing precisely due to its aesthetic dimension: since it has no connection to reality, mathematics “must be justified as art if it can be justified as all.”

>[!quote] Hardy, *A Mathematician’s Apology* (quoted in [[@2018engelhardtModernism]])
>I am interested in mathematics only as a  creative art’, Hardy declares and proclaims both to share a common aesthetics, claiming that  ‘[t]he mathematician’s patterns, like the painter’s or the poet’s, must be beautiful [...]. Beauty is  the first test: there is no permanent place in the world for ugly mathematics.

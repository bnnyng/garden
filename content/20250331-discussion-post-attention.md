# Discussion post

I think that the theory of attention as ideational preparation/anticipatory imagination is most consistent with classical behavioral and neuroscientific studies (e.g., neural activity being correlated with reward prediction error, babies staring longer at something that is surprising). I do not quite see how to square this with intellectual attention in a DoGMA-friendly way. Buckner discuss its potential utility as a solution to the Control Problem on page 259, but where is ideational preparation rel “endogenously generated ideas” coming from? 

It’s also worth carefully distinguishing between the problems of *directing* attention vs. *sustaining* attention. For example, by definition, surprise directs but does not sustain attention. I think of sustained attention as the behavior we would call “interest” in ordinary language, which can be attention sustained over a single task period (maybe with some notion of “inertia”) or a repeated pattern of attention associated with an agent. The focus of Buckner’s discussion is on the former (which can be described in functional terms, while the latter is mostly teleological/behavioral) but I imagine we will also end up talking about the latter.

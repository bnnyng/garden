# Construction and basic definition

>[!theorem] [[MATH-GU4042|Modern Algebra II]] HW 2.2
>If $R$ is a [[Algebraic rings|ring with unity]], then there is a *unique* [[Ring homomorphisms and isomorphisms|homomorphism]] $f : \mathbb Z \to R$ defined by $$ f(n) = n \cdot 1. $$ Further, the image of $f$ is the cyclic subgroup generated by $1 \in \mathbb Z$ and *isomorphic* (as a ring) to either $\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb Z / n \mathbb Z$ for some $d \in \mathbb Z$ (see also: [[Integers modulo n]]).

>[!definition] Characteristic of an integral domain
>Let $R$ be an [[Cancellation laws and integral domains|integral domain]]. The **characteristic** of $R$ is zero if $1 \in R$ has infinite order, and $p$ if $1 \in R$ has finite order of some prime $p$. 

>[!exm] 
>If $R \leq S$ is a [[Algebraic rings|subring]], then $\textup{char }R = \textup{char }S$. Thus, the fact that $\textup{char } \mathbb Z = 0$ implies that $\textup{char } \mathbb Q = \textup{char } \mathbb C = \textup{char } \mathbb Z [ \sqrt 2] = \textup{char } \mathbb Q (\sqrt[3]2) = \cdots$, etc. are all $0$ as well.

# Properties

#### Fields of characteristic zero

- [[Formal derivatives on polynomial rings]]: If $f \in F[x]$ is **irreducible** over $F$, then $f$ has **no repeated roots** in *any* extension $E$ of $F$. This is because an irreducible polynomial $f$ has repeated roots in some extension if and only if $Df = 0$. However, in characteristic zero, if $f = \sum a_i x^i$ and hence $Df = \sum i a_i x^{i-1}$, we have $$ f \neq 0 \implies a_i \neq 0 \text{ for some }i \implies i a_i \neq 0 \implies Df \neq 0. $$

Hence, every finite **normal** extension of $F$ is a **Galois** extension. (See also: [[Perfect fields#Connections to other topics]].)

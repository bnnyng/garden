[[@1907russell]] proposes the following “**regressive**” method of determining the true [[The axiomatic method in mathematics|axioms]] of mathematics: we begin with [[Mathematical intuition|intuitions]], or plausibility judgements, and then [[Abduction is going from a believed conclusion to requirements for the conclusion to be true, after Peirce|abduct]] to the *best* axiomatization of those judgements. This mirrors how [[Laws and causation in scientific explanation|laws of nature]] are determined in the sciences.

# Selected passages

- “The usual mathematical method of laying down certain premises and proceeding to deduce their consequences, though it is the right method of exposition, does not, except in the more advanced portions, give the order of knowledge. This has been concealed by the fact that the propositions traditionally taken as premises are for the most part very obvious, with the fortunate exception of the axiom of parallels. But when we push analysis farther, and get to more ultimate premises, the obviousness becomes less, and the analogy with the procedure of other sciences becomes more visible.”

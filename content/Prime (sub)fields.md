# Overview and basic definition

As a consequence of the [[Categories and morphisms|homomorphism]] induced on the [[Field of quotients of an integral domain|field of quotients]] on any [[Algebraic fields|field]] $F$, we can identify the *unique* minimal subfield of $F$ called the **prime subfield**, which is generated by its multiplicative identity $1 \in F$. Every field $F$ has a unique subfield isomorphic to $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$, depending on its [[Characteristics of rings|characteristic]].

>[!example] Definition: Prime (sub)field
>Let $F$ be a [[Algebraic fields|field]] and $f: \mathbb Z \to F$ be the unique [[Ring homomorphisms and isomorphisms|homomorphism]] defined by $f(n) = n \cdot 1$.
>- If $F$ has [[Characteristics of rings|characteristic]] $0$, then $f$ is [[Injectivity, surjectivity, and bijectivity|injective]] and induces an injective homomorphism $\tilde f : \mathbb Q \to F$. In particular, the image of $f$ the smallest [[Mathematical communication is most effective in subfields due to shared patterns of thinking|subfield]] of $F$ (hence *unique*) and isomorphic to $\mathbb Q$.
>- If $F$ has positive characteristic $p$, then $f$ has kernel $\langle p \rangle$ and hence image isomorphic to $\mathbb Z / p \mathbb Z = \mathbb F_p$, the ring of [[Integers modulo n|integers modulo]] prime $p$.
>
>The *unique* subfields isomorphic to $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb F_p$ for fields of characteristic $0$ and $p$, respectively, are called **prime subfields**. The fields $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb F_p$ are themselves called **prime fields** and contain no proper subfields.

# Properties

- [[Extension fields]]: Every field is an **extension** of its prime subfield.
- [[Finite extension fields]]: Every *finite* field (which has characteristic prime $p > 0$) arises as a [[Extension fields|simple extension]] of its prime subfield $\mathbb F = \mathbb F_p(\gamma)$, where $\gamma$ is a [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|generator]] of the cyclic group $(\mathbb F^*, \cdot)$.

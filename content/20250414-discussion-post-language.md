# Discussion post

I wonder how we should interpret the LLM-based artificial agents described at the end of Milliere & Buckner’s Part II, particularly the “generative agents” in Park (2023) which can plausibly simulate human behavior like information diffusion, relationship formation, and coordination, given some well-chosen seed prompts. This is precisely an example of robot agents using human culture—the paper is published under HCI, so presumably the applications are in interactive programs where the emergence of an unfamiliar culture would be undesirable. Thus, as a mechanistic model of human (social!) behaviors, these agents must be a proof-of-concept. 

It seems that our class is generally more troubled by artificial agency than artificial intelligence, or pure problem-solving abilities—teleonomy vs computation. Milliere & Buckner point out that individual generative agents don’t exhibit intrinsic goals, only induced goals from the developer’s prompt, but aside from basic survival needs I would say that my own intrinsic goals are largely “downloaded” from collective culture (itself contingent/path-dependent). How are these different? Thank goodness for the constraints of HCI and capitalism; if these agents were permitted to develop an independent culture and demonstrate some kind of “ratcheting”, I would be hard pressed to say why we shouldn’t consider them on par with humans.

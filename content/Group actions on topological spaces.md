# General group actions in topological spaces

A group action on a topological space $Y$ is a subgroup of the **topological symmetries** of $Y$—the group of [[Homeomorphisms and topological embeddings|homeomorphisms]] $\textup{Homeo}(Y)$ from a space $Y$ to itself.

>[!definition] Group action on a topological space
>If $G$ is a [[Algebraic groups|group]] and $Y$ is any space, a **group action** $G \times Y \to Y$ is a [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|group homomorphism]] from $G$ to the group $\text{Homeo}(Y)$ of [[Homeomorphisms and topological embeddings|homeomorphisms]] from $Y$ to itself. Explicitly, each element of $G$ is associated with a homeomorphism $g: Y \to Y$ with mapping and inverse defined, respectively by
>$$
>y \mapsto g \cdot y \quad \quad y \mapsto g^{-1} \cdot y.
>$$

>[!definition] Orbits of a group action on a topological space
>Given an action of a group $G$ on a space $Y$, the **orbit space** $Y / G$ of the action is the [[Quotient spaces|quotient space]] formed by identifying $y \sim g \cdot y$. The points of $Y/G$ are the **orbits**
>$$
>Gy = \{ g \cdot y \ | \ g \in G\}.
>$$

# Continuous actions

>[!definition] Continuous action
>Let $G$ be a [[Algebraic groups|group]] and $X$ be a [[Topological spaces and open sets|topological space]]. A **continuous (left) action** of $G$ on $X$ is a map $\alpha : G \times X \to X$ that is [[Continuous functions|continuous]] when $G$ is equipped with the [[Discrete topology|discrete topology]] and also a [[Group actions and orbits|(left) action]] on the underlying set, i.e., $$ \alpha(g_2g_1, x) = \alpha(g_2, \alpha(g_1, x))$$ for all $g_1, g_2 \in G$ and $x \in X$. For simplicity, the action is often written $\alpha(g, x) = g \cdot x$.

>[!lemma] [[MATH-GU4053|Algebraic Topology]] HW 3.3: Elements in a continuous group action correspond with homeomorphisms
>Let $G$ be a group and $\varphi : G \to \textup{Homeo}(Y)$ be a [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|group homomorphism]], where $\textup{Homeo}(Y)$ is the group of [[Homeomorphisms and topological embeddings|homeomorphisms]] $Y \to Y$. Then the mapping $G \times X \to Y$ defined by $$(g, y) \mapsto \varphi(g)(y) $$ is a continuous action of $G$ on $Y$. 
>
>Conversely, any continuous action of $G$ on $Y$ arises in this way for a uniquely determined group homomorphism $\varphi : G \to \textup{Homeo}(Y)$.

>[!exm]
>The set of all [[Homeomorphisms and topological embeddings|homeomorphisms]] $X \to X$ of a space $X$ forms a group with respect to composition, which acts on $X$ in the obvious way: if $f: X \to X$ is a homeomorphism, then $f \cdot x = f(x)$. The subgroup generated by any (finite or infinite) set of homeomorphisms $X \to X$ also acts on $X$ in this way.

# Covering maps from group actions

>[!definition] Covering space action
>A **covering space action** $G \times Y \to Y$ is a continuous action of a group $G$ on a space $Y$ such that for all $y \in Y$, there exists a neighborhood $U \subseteq Y$ such that the images for different $g \in G \backslash \{ e \}$ are disjoint: $$ g_1(U)\cap g_2(U) \neq \varnothing \implies g_1 = g_2. $$

>[!lemma]
>If $G \times Y \to Y$ is a continuous action, then the canonical [[Quotient spaces|quotient map]] $p : Y \to Y / G$ is a [[Covering maps|covering map]] *if and only if*  the action is a covering space action.

The identity $ST = TS^{-1}$ implies any element of $G$ can be written as an element $T^a, S^b$ for $a, b \in \mathbb Z$, so the map $\varphi: \mathbb Z^2 \to G$ defined by $(a, b) \mapsto T^aS^b$ is surjective. After showing that $\varphi$ is also injective, we conclude that $\varphi$ is a bijection of sets.

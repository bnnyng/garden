# Discussion post

I am somewhat confused by the scope of discussion in Cowie’s article, since most of the example concepts she uses to critique Fodor’s nativism, such as PLATYPUS and MONOTREME, are quite particular. I was under the impression that the innateness debate was particularly focused on whether it was theoretically consistent to assume some distinguished conceptual *primitives*, such as AGENT and NUMBER and other alleged “core knowledge” systems mentioned in class (or maybe I’m just projecting, since I am so convinced PLATYPUS and MONOTREME are not innate that I am just dismissing their discussion). But I also don’t quite understand how AGENT and NUMBER count as concepts in the Fodorian sense, since they their role in cognition seems to be providing a pragmatic context to make the computational inferences that lead to concept learning. 

Another point of confusion for me is that concepts seem a bit ill-defined within cognition itself. What if I initially misrepresent some token of a concept, and later correct it? The only reason I have to believe that these still fall under the same concept is that I have a mental symbol (e.g., an exemplar, the actual name of the concept) that I have decided to port over to the new representation. Is learning a new subject (in the ordinary sense) just a Theseus’s ship situation with the relevant concepts?

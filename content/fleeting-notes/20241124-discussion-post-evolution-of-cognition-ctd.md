# Discussion post

Kydd posits that the uniquely human ability to attend to seemingly arbitrary ideas—the basis of human exceptionalism, which we might more properly call the [[Human cultural learning shows a unique ratcheting effect, after Tomasello|ratchet effect]], according to everyone form Aristotle to [[@2015han]]—was a product of “supernatural” [[Humans are the niche constructors par excellence, after Kydd|niche construction]], particularly decoupling representations from physical reality by binding them together in cognition. Further, the niche-construction behaviors are (1) culturally-modulated manipulations of the dopaminergic system, a primary neural basis for motivation and (2) “specific cognitive endophenotypes,” which is a term I am not familiar with but I take to mean the things we would call psychiatric illnesses today. Tangentially, does it follow from behavior (1) that everything is just reinforcement learning?

I’m reminded of pop-science theories that ADHD would have been evolutionarily advantageous in hunter-gatherer times, or that people with psychosis would be considered mystics in another cultural context. I always saw these as a sort of cope: psychiatry is a construct, yes, but we exist in a context where these things are no longer adaptive advantages. Also, perhaps our current environment selects for genes that enable fragmented attention and multitasking (according to [[@2015han]], a regression to the behavior of wild animals), rather than deep contemplation.

I also don’t really know what to make of the “brilliance-prone spectrum,” particularly how the brilliant/psychotic trait is indeed a trait (that is, distinct from “normal” quantities of creativity and psychosis). I think the term brilliant is misleading, since it implies some notion of “genius,” which I do not love (this then fits with popular narratives of the mad genius; see: either of Benjamin Labatut’s books). I recently read an essay by Terrance Tao where he describes mathematical discovery as, in a sense, inevitable, since any major advancement is deduced from a crowdsourced collection of atomic results; it only takes an individual who can put together the zeitgeist. I think the cognitive attributes required for such an individual would be something like degrees of freedom in ideation, mental representations that are less grounded in typical “cognitive bindings.” 

Finally, Simone Weil’s philosophy feels relevant: “Attention, taken to its highest degree, is the same thing as prayer. It presupposes faith and love. Absolutely unmixed attention is prayer.”

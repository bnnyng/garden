# Discussion post

- Identify the coarsest units we could get away with
- Self-organization doesn’t come from the level of modules and connections
	- Modify their quantity and connection

It is curious that Mitchell refers to morphogenesis as a “series of mindless biochemical reactions, with each cell reacting to signals from outside it,” immediately after comparing neurons to basal cognitive systems like amoebas and whatnot. I suppose interrogating anthropocentric bias is outside the scope of this chapter but the term “mindless”, to me, immediately begs the question of where to draw the line between cognitive and non-cognitive systems. It seems far easier to consider cognition on a continuum. There are certainly unifying principles between myself and a neuron, and for all of its weaknesses, behaviorism at least accommodated comparative cognition in a way that specifying mental mechanisms does not: both myself and the cells pursue goals and depend on feedback to maintain homeostasis. 

I also appreciated the discussion of robustness and self-similar levels of organization, though I think the connection could have been made more explicit. Mitchell seemed to treat the relation between genetics and behavior as a black box, unlike the “simple” relationships between a sickle cell, for example, and the mutation that caused it. But it is clear that this is simply because behavior is several levels of organization removed: the basic unit of a cell consists of a primary autocatalytic network that synthesizes DNA proteins to perform replication, along with some auxiliary enzymes that aid its metabolism. Something like sickle cell disease is a first-order effect, a mutated network that achieved homeostasis, despite being probabilistically implausible, and hence could produce persistent copies of itself. But, before we get to behavior we have to go through neural-level circuits, anatomical substructures, the brain in its totality, the body with the metabolism to support it, and whatever other dozen levels you want to carve in between. I feel that Mitchell did not fully explain away the magic of “robustness”—for example, how embryogenesis happens consistently even with all these variations and mutations stacked against it—but there *is* some [principled (non-random) chaos](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/short-time-attractors)going on.

Given that our best examples of robustness are outcomes of collective dynamics, this does not look great for the DoGMA recommendation to get rational, robust AI via artisanal mental modules. On the other hand, we certainly don’t need to lay out all the details of quantum field theory to model a cell. Presumably there is a sweet spot where we identify the coarsest fundamental units that we can get away with, but I certainly don’t think those units are the mental modules like “attention” lol (layers of neural networks, being, in general, arrays and tensors, do not have any autocatalytic properties).

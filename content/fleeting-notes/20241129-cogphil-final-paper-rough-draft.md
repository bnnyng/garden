#### Introduction

- Nothing more example of unique nature of humans than mathematical reasoning, abstract reasoning capacities
	- But within mathematical communities, reasoning necessary but not sufficient part of being a mathematician—something elusive *je ne sais quoi* called “intuition”
- Social reasoning hypothesis can help explain these aspects of mathematical practice
	- Understanding mathematical practice from this view can also say interesting things about intelligence; i.e., what becomes possible after intuition is acquired, what is its use

#### Social reasoning hypothesis
- Reasoning enables coordination between acquaintances
	- Transmit correct beliefs
- Mathematics is also unavoidably social
	- A good notion of ground truth in math is what the larger community of mathematicians believes at any given time
#### Cognitive signposts – what is the role of words in thought?

- Sort of to justify where a cognitive perspective on mathematics comes from, aside from raw intelligence, etc. 
	- “Intimately tied to the notion of language as a cognitive signpost”
	- Math can serve as its own referents
	- “Autodidact wrestling with disembodied mathematical concepts”; a purely individual exercise
- Cognitive signposts, quote from Stella Maris—words are a part of our thought
	- Also note that artifacts of mathematical practice are proofs and papers; implicit in this is a proof—to whom? A way of communication.
	- Cognitive signposts make non-physical objects into things we can perform inferences over
		- Making thoughts into entities—clearly a basic part of intangible mathematical practice
- Where do the underlying meanings come from?
	- Social reasoning hypothesis—the “natural form” of reasoning/rationalizing/articulating reasons in general is based on social need
#### Mathematical intuition

- Math is “inevitable” because it’s built up of atomic facts, just takes a “visionary”
	- In the language of cognitive science, it is perceiving “affordances”
- Compare to autodidactic automated theorem provers

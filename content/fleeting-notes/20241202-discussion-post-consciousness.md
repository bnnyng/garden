# Discussion post

I’ve never really been interested in consciousness due to the notion of intrinsic subjectivity, i.e. “a subjective dimension that is not the product of introspective mechanisms but arises simply from our being the things we are,” always seeming somewhat silly to me. I guess I’ve just been some flavor of illusionist this whole time? In particular, I agree with the theory that what we interpret as consciousness is some kind of introspective representational awareness. This allows for the feeling of being more or less conscious of different things at different times, a continuum that seems hard to justify if you are a realist; what would it mean for some phenomena to be more or less conscious? Further, I think (similar to theory referenced in the reading from Humphrey, 2011?) self-attention maintains itself recursively. We generate “places to stand” in our cognition, which are real in the same way affordances are real, and enabled by a base starter pack of entities like pronunciation re: Jackendoff.

One aspect of the illusionist perspective I disagree with is the claim introspection distorts our experiences by “misrepresenting complex physical features as simple phenomenal ones.” This seems to trivialize the products of introspection, but artifacts of introspection are clearly useful if we can still use them to function well in the physical world. Introspection seems to at least capture the motivationally salient relationships between different physical things; and then isn’t this just a relevant coarse-graining? I think of physical reality as a sort of Lacanian Real that must be transformed by several cognitive filters, from the information we can physically sense, to their perceptual form, and the various degrees of recursion that determine the place of that information in awareness.

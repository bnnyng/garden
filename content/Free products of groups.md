# Overview

The **free product** is an operation on [[Algebraic groups|groups]] $G, H$ that constructs a new group denoted $G * H$ with the following [[Universal properties of topologies|universal property]]:
- (i) There exist injective [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|homomorphisms]] $i_j : G_j \to G_1 * G_2$ for $j = 1, 2$, so each $G_j$ is identified with a subgroup of $G_1 * G_2$;
- (ii) Given a group $J$ and homomorphisms $\varphi_j : G _j \to H$ for $j = 1, 2$, there exists a *unique* homomorphism $\varphi : G_1 * G_2 \to H$ such that $\varphi \circ i_j = \varphi_j$ for $j = 1, 2$ (the free product is precisely the [[Disjoint unions|coproduct]] in the category of non-abelian groups.

# Free products


>[!example] Definition: Free product of groups
>Given [[Algebraic groups|groups]] $G_1, G_2$, their **free product** $G_1 * G_2$ is constructed as the set of **reduced [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|words]]**, or finite products $g_1g_2\cdots g_n$ where:
>- Each $g_i$ is in some $G_i$;
>- None are equal to the identity (i.e., $g_i \neq e_j \in G_j$ for all $i,j$);
>- Adjacent generators $g_i, g_{i+1}$ belong to different groups $G_j$ for all $i$.
>
>The group operation is concatenation of reduced words
>$$
>(g_1 \cdots g_n)(h_1 \cdots h_n) = g_1\cdots g_n h_1 \cdots h_n.
>$$
>Note that if $g_n, h_1$ are in the same group $G_j$, we replace them with their product $(g_n h_1) \in G_j$ or erase it if $(g_n h_1) = e$. The identity is the empty word, and inverses are defined by
>$$
>(g_1 \cdots g_n)^{-1} = g_n^{-1}g_{n-1}^{-1} \cdots g_1^{-1}.
>$$

# Free groups

>[!example] Definition: Free group
>Let $G$ be a group and $\{ g_\alpha \}_{\alpha \in J}$ be a family of elements of $G$. If each $g_\alpha$ [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|generates]] an *infinite* [[Cyclic groups|cyclic]] [[Algebraic groups|subgroup]] $G_\alpha \leq G$ and $G$ is the free product of the groups $\{ G_\alpha \}$, then $G$ is said to be a **free group** and the family $\{ g_\alpha \}_{\alpha \in J}$  is a **system of free generators** for $G$.

# Examples

#### Free product of integers

Suppose $\mathbb Z * \mathbb Z$ has the first copy of $\mathbb Z$ generated by $a$, i.e.,
$$
G_1 = \mathbb Z \cong \{ a^n \ | \ n \in \mathbb Z\},
$$
and the second copy $G_2$ have elements generated by $b$. Elements of the free product $\mathbb Z * \mathbb Z$ have the form $a^8b^{-1}ab^4$, etc., and multiplication looks like 
$$
(ba^3b^5) \cdot (b^4a) = ba^3b^9a.
$$

#### Free group of rank $n$

>[!example] Definition: Free group of rank $n$
>The **free group of rank $n$** is the group defined by
>$$
>F_n = \mathbb Z * \cdots * \mathbb Z,
>$$
>the free product of $n$ copies of $\mathbb Z$.

# Notes

- Motivation: What is the [[Fundamental groups|fundamental group]] of $G = \pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1, x_0)$, where $S^1 \vee S^1$ is two circles intersecting at a single point? Recall that [[(Theorem) The fundamental group of the circle is isomorphic to the additive group of integers]], so two embeddings of $S^1 \to S_1 \vee S^1$ give two copies of $\mathbb Z \subseteq G$. Note that $G \neq \mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z$ since these copies do not commute. 
- The result $G * H$ contains both $G, H$ as subgroups and is [[Group generators, relations, and presentations|generated by]] elements of these groups. The free product is finite if and only if one of $G, H$ is trivial (🔺 why?).

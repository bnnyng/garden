[[@2004penroseRoad]] argues for the following relationship between three kinds of existence, whose entities inhabit distinct “worlds.”

![[Pasted image 20250420111638.png|300]] 

In this figure, we see that:
- Only a small part of the Platonic mathematical world is relevant to the physical world. On the other hand, the entire physical world is *governed* by precisely by mathematical laws, typically what we would now call equations; our actions are subject to what Penrose calls “mathematical control.”
- Only a small minority of physical structures induce mentality. On the other hand, all mental events have a basis in physical events—this makes Penrose a [[Classical theories of mind|reductionist]] about psychology.
- Only a small part of mental activity is about mathematical truth. On the other hand, there are no mathematical truths outside of the scope of human reason or insight, *in principle* (possibly related: [[Undecidability and incompleteness theorems]]).

While the connections between these worlds remains “mysterious,” Penrose holds that a view in which one world does not project completely onto another would raise far more puzzles.

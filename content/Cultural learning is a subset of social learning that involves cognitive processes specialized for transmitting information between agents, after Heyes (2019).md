$\quad$ [[@2019heyesCognitive]] gives the following taxonomy for **learning**, broadly defined as encoding for long-term storage of information that is acquired through experience. **Social learning**, in contrast with asocial or **individual learning**, is learning that is assisted by other agents.

![[Pasted image 20250729162748.png|400]]

$\quad$ **Cultural learning** is “a subset of social learning involving cognitive processes that are specialized for cultural evolution.” Common types of cultural learning cited by theories of cultural evolution include:

- **Selective social learning:** also known as **learning biases**, **transmission biases**, **social learning rules**, and **social learning strategies**; when the influence of a “model” or “demonstrator” agent varies with the circumstances of the learning encounter;
- **(True) imitation:** when observing a model causes the observer to perform “topographically similar behavior,” or “behavior in which parts of the observer’s body move in the same way, relative to one another, as parts of the model’s body”;
- **Mindreading:** also known as [[Theory-of-mind|theory-of-mind]], **mentalizing**, **shared intentionality**, and **folk psychology**; the ascription of mental states, and likely the key factor for effective **teaching**;
- **Language**.

$\quad$[[@2019heyesCognitive]] argues that many of these examples of cultural learning are socially acquired “[[Distinctly human cognitive mechanisms arise from selection on cultural variants, after Heyes (2019)|cognitive gadgets]]” that draw upon domain-general faculties, rather than being genetically determined instincts.

# Key terms

- **Inference** = any process of extracting new information from information that is already available; **reasoning** is the process where information is extracted by “attending to reasons.”
	- **Inferential procedure** = a procedure that has the function of making more information available for processing, or making available information more reliable.
- **Cognitive module** = in contrast to controversial history of the word, [[@2017mercier]] define this as biological modules (e.g., the fusiform face area) with a cognitive function.
- **Representation** = a physical item that has the function of providing an information-processing device with some information about the state of the world.

# Reading notes

## Understanding inference

- **Three basic properties of all cognitive systems:**
	- **Goal-directed locomotion:** “Cognition is first and foremost a means for organisms that can move around to react appropriately to risks and opportunities presented by their environment.”
	- **Inferring beyond sensory information:** “All that the sensory organs get by way of information, be it in ants or in humans, are changes of energy at thousands or millions of nerve endings. To integrate this information, to identify the events in the environment that have caused these sensory stimulations, to respond in an appropriate manner to these events, cognition must, to a large extent, consist in drawing inferences about the way things are, what to expect, and about what to do.”
	- **Inference is not necessarily domain-general:** “Inferences may be performed by specialized mechanisms that are each remarkably good at dealing with just one quite specific task: inferring distance on the basis of a number of steps, etc.”
- **Nature and nurture on a continuum:** “A learning instinct, as \[Marler] meant it, isn’t an indiscriminate disposition to learn anything; it is an evolved instinct to acquire a given type of knowledge, such as songs (for birds) or language (for humans). A learning instinct not only targets a specific learning goal, it also provides the instinctive learner with appropriate perceptual and inferential mechanisms to extract the right kind of knowledge from the right kind of evidence.”
	- “What makes Marler’s idea of a ‘learning instinct’ a source of insight is that it suggests, rather than a gap, there can be a continuum of cases between wholly evolved instincts at one end and wholly acquired expertises at the other end. Cognitive mechanisms may occupy various positions on this continuum.”
- **Cognitive modularity is neither innate nor rigid:** “Reading … is a perfect illustration of a cognitive module realized in brain tissues, the biologically evolved properties of which leant themselves to being recycled for a novel, culturally evolved function” (see also: [[Neural expatation]]; [[@2005dehaene]]).

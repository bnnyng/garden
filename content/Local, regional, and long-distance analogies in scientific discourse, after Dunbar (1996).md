$\quad$ In a study of the mechanisms underlying conceptual change and insight, [[@1996dunbarHow]] identifies three types of analogies used during laboratory meetings among molecular biologists.

| Type of analogy | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                     | Example                                                                                                                                               | Occurrence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Local           | An analogical mapping between the current problem and a similar experiment that was successful, where components from the successful approach were substituted into the unsuccessful solution. |                                                                                                                                                       | When an experiment was not working: “New knowledge is added to their representation by making the analogy, and this drives the research forward.”                                                                                                                                                  |
| Regional        | A mapping over *systems* of relationships in two different domains that fall under a shared superordinate category.                                                                            | Both phage viruses and retroviruses are types of virus, and the one can be better understood by trying to map knowledge about the other.              | When elaborating a scientific theory and planning new experiments, *after* a system of relations has already been built up in one domain: “This type of analogy tended to be used only after the scientists started to formulate a model of the entire process that they were investigating.”      |
| Long distance   | A mapping between two largely unrelated domains.                                                                                                                                               | Describing the flagellar pocket: “Things get in, but things don't... It's like the Hotel California - you can check in, but you can never check out.” | When highlighting salient features of research, emphasizing a point, or or changing the representation of knowledge in others (e.g., during education).<br><br>[[@1996dunbarHow]] did *not* observe instances where long-distance analogies led to conceptual change or insights for a researcher. |

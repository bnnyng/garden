# Overview

$\quad$**Inferential social learning**, after [[@2021gweonInferential]], is a framework that characterizes human *social* learning as drawing inferences from evidence generated by observing and interacting with others, just as *individual* learning involves observing physical events and self-generating evidence via [[Exploration-exploitation tradeoff|exploration]].

![[Pasted image 20250716132107.png|450]]

$\quad$ Making such inferences requires a *generative model of other minds*, which includes not only subjective mental states (i.e., [[Theory-of-mind|theory-of-mind]]), but also costs and rewards (e.g., [[The Naive Utility Calculus, after Jara-Ettinger et al. (2016)|Naive Utility Calculus]]).

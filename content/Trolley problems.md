# Overview

$\quad$ The **trolley problem** is a well-known [[Thought experiments|thought experiment]] in ethics. In the most basic version, a trolley is heading towards five people on a track, and the decision-making [[Agent|agent]] must decide whether to switch the track so that the trolley will head towards a single person instead. From [[@2020railtonEthical]]:

>Trolley problems have sometimes been called the Drosophila of ethical inquiry—a shared, heavily studied “test bed” for hypotheses about ethical judgment. It is, moreover, a nice irony that trolley problems, long castigated by critics as hopelessly artificial, turn out to have such direct analogues in one of the most important actual applications of artificial intelligence to life-affecting decision-making to date: self-driving vehicles. (59)

# Variants of the trolley problem

| Variant name | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Typical responses                               |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Switch       | The trolley track splits so that one person is lying on the track on one side, and five people are lying on the track on the other side. The trolley is currently positioned to hit and kill the five people, and a decision-making agent must decide whether to switch the track so that the trolley only kills the one person.         | Strong majority (80 percent) says to switch     |
| Footbridge   | The decision-making agent is located on a footbridge directly above the track. Instead of deciding whether to pull a switch that will kill one person instead of five, the agent must decide whether to push a large man from the footbridge onto the track so that the trolley hits the single man instead of running over five people. | Strong majority (75 percent) say not to switch  |
| Loop         | Instead of splitting evenly in two, the track has a side “loop” that will circle back to the main track, and there is a large man sitting on the loop. If the agent switches the track, the trolley will hit and kill the large man, but be stopped before it can reach the five people on the main track.                               | Strong majority say switch to the looping track |
| Beckon       | The agent can beckon to a large man on the opposite side of the track so that he steps in front of the trolley and dies, but stops the trolley before it reaches five others.                                                                                                                                                            | Slight majority say do *not* beckon             |
| Wave         | The agent can wave to the five people on the track to encourage them to step aside. However, there is a sixth person behind them who will also heed the wave and step onto the track to be hit by the trolley.                                                                                                                           | Strong majority say to wave                     |

Grice’s theory of **pragmatic reasoning** in discourse takes the relationship between speakers and listeners to be the following:
- **Speakers** are cooperative and choose their utterances to convey particular meanings
- **Listeners** infer the speaker’s intended communicative goal by working backward from the utterance’s form. 

Grice proposes a set of **conversational maxims** that underly behavior in discourse: be truthful, relevant, informative, and perspicuous. The speaker’s behavior *relative* to these maxims then leads to **implicatures**, or inferences about their intended meaning.

The conversational maxims take language to be a [[Rationality in cognitive science|rational]] action. This is quantified in a probabilistic model by [[(Model) The Rational Speech Acts framework, after Frank and Goodman (2016)]].

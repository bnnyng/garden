#### Audition

**Parts of the ear**

- Outer ear ends at the tympanic membrane; mid ear has three important bones (**ossicles**); inner ear
	- **Ossicles:** malleus, incus, and stapes increase the vibrations from the tympanic membrane by a factor of 200
- The **cochlea** is the “retina of audition”; inside of the spiral corresponds to lower Hz
	- Also calculates orientation via three-dimensional structures called **vestibules**
	- The cochlear “tube” has three chambers (**scalae**), two filled with calcium ions and one filled with potassium → “where the neural work starts” (**organ of Corti**), analogous to going from cones to basal ganglia

**Mechanisms of audition**

- “Everything happens in the link between hair cells”; hair cells start vibrating based on position in the cochlea, and start vibrating at different speeds
	- Filaments (?) record differences between hair vibrations, mechanisms → electricity → auditory cortex
- **Cadherins** are proteins that help cells hold to one another, here serve as particular filaments connecting hair cells 
	- Most common cause of hereditary deafness, cause of noise-induced hearing loss

**Neural pathway for audition**

- From cochlea, impulse goes to the **medulla** at the base of the brainstem → **pons**, further up on the brainstem → inferior colliculus → medial geniculate body → auditory cortex
- Superior olivary nucleus performs **source localization** from interaural differences

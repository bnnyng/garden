# Week 8, Day 2: Dreams
---

**Review: Memory and the hippocampus**

- According to the **complementary learning systems hypothesis**, learning and memory storage happens in two separate brain areas: the subcortical **hippocampus** and the **cortex**.
	- **Rapid learning** of direct, unfiltered experience occurs in the hippocampus, which then facilitates consolidation of information into the rest of the cortex in a way that prevents **catastrophic interference**.
	- The cortex store general, long-term knowledge of the world.
- **Catastrophic interference** involves the failure to preserve information about previous contexts after encountering a new task. For example, overwriting weights in a vanilla neural network during training.
- The hippocampus appears to help consolidate information in the cortex by **interleaving** new and old information.

**Theories of dreams**

- **Residue theory:** after Freud; the content of dreams arises from the unconscious, but without a central meaningful narrative. Analyzing dreams is like going through someone’s trash—you can learn something, but it is not an intentionally constructed artifact.
- **Evolutionary theory:** dreams about monsters in particular arise from predators of humans in evolutionary history.
- **Complementary learning systems theory:** dreams allow for “offline” practicing and learning that helps memory consolidation.
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ After cramming for a test, you will have a better chance of remembering information you studied on the following day if you do *not* sleep.
- **Jungian theory:** dreams are a paradigmatic stage at which the unconscious communicates to the conscious. 

**Dreams and the unconscious**

- For psychoanalysts who believe in a **pressure theory** of dreams, the fact that cannabis withdrawal leads to vivid dreams could be explained by cannabis being a psychoactive substance that dissolves the boundary between unconscious and conscious, and without the drug, the unconscious releases “pressure” during nightly dreams.
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Are the different theories compatible? Which are compatible with the **complementary learning systems** theory?

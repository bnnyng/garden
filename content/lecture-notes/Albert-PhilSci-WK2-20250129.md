# Week 2, Day 2: Probabilistic Explanations in Science
---

**What is a probability?**

- From last time ([[Albert-PhilSci-WK2-20250127|Week 2, Day 1: Scientific Explanations]]): What does it mean to say, “This weighted coin has a 0.6 chance of landing ‘heads’”? For example, how would you explain this statement to a Martian?
- The most sophisticated **frequentist** attempt defines a probability as the **limiting value** of the ratio of heads to tales after $n$ flips, as $n \to \infty$. This view is also called **naive frequentist probability**.
- The fundamental issue with **subjective probability** is that it is not obvious why the meaning of the claim “0.6 chance of landing ‘heads’” should have anything to do with rational agents.
	- In **subjective subjectivism**, probability has different values for different people.
- The third view is probability as a **logical primitive**: the concept of probability cannot be restated isomorphically in other terms.
	- Albert characterizes this view as dismissing analysis: “You’ll know it when you see it.”

|                                                                                              | Frequentism                                                                               | Subjectivism                                                                                             | Primitivism                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| *Interpretation of the statement, “The weighted coin will land heads with probability 0.6.”* | Limiting value of the ratio of heads to tails as the number of flips approaches infinity. | A rational agent (objective) or I (subjective) would bet 60 cents to a dollar on the coin landing heads. | Probability is an irreducible logical primitive, hence cannot be translated into other concepts. |
| *Main limitations*                                                                           | Every attempt to define probability includes notion of probability                        | Unclear why this should be a good *meaning* for the probability statement                                | Unsatisfying                                                                                     |
| *Makes claim about a fact of the matter in the world*                                        | ✔️                                                                                        | ✕                                                                                                        | ✔️                                                                                               |
| *Can be analyzed*                                                                            | ✔️                                                                                        | ✔️                                                                                                       | ✕                                                                                                |

**Why are Hempel’s criteria for good scientific explanations unsatisfying?**

- We want scientific explanations to be more than just a collection of facts—we want explanation of **cause**.
	- *Example.* $\text{ }$ “Why is the tree that high?” An unsatisfying explanation would be computing the height of the tree based on knowledge of the hour of the day and the length of its shadow. A satisfying answer would probably have something to do with botany.
- *Discussion.* $\text{ }$ What is a cause?
	- Something that causes a phenomenon must at least be **temporally antecedent** to that phenomenon.

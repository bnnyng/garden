# Week 11, Day 1: Attention
---

**The control problem**

- The **control problem** refers to cognitive systems handle competition for resources and clashes between outputs of different faculties. This gives a concrete starting point for a theory of **attention**: a solution to the control problem.

**Attention as ideational preparation**

- One feature of attention is that we can attend to things that isn’t directly the focus of some perceptual faculties. For example, we can attend to an object in peripheral vision without moving the eyes. 
- According to William James, while this **organic adjustment** (i.e., Jamesian **overt attention**) is a form of attention, attention also includes **ideational preparation** (i.e., Jamesian **covert attention**) that is not a direct response to some bottom-up signal.

**Models of attention**

- **Region proposals** for CNNs mimic saccadic eye movements by selecting **salient** areas of an image for more focused processing.
	- Early R-CNNs used a hard-coded algorithm for detecting regions—a more rationalist model.
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Is the very specific design choice required for R-CNNs a form of nativist bias for certain conceptual items in the environment?
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ Is the figure-background distinction learned or innate? Is it really a gestalt principle, or just an artistic convention?

**Presentation: Parallel Distributed Processing at 25**

- **Parallel distributed processing (PDP)** is the theory that cognitive systems process information simultaneously across a network of units, rather than sequentially.
	- Knowledge is encoded across multiple units.
	- Learning occurs when weights of units are adjusted.
- PDP systems can model incremental learning in children, as well as memory degradation (e.g., gradual weakening of weights of times) and retrieval patterns.
	- However, their actual correspondence with biological information processing in brains is unclear.
- In the future, PDP models can be used to study neurological disorders.

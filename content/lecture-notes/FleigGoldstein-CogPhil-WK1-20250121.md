# Week 1, Day 1: Introduction
---

- During the “Scientific Revolution,” what constitutes **scientific knowledge** goes from Aristotle’s “natural philosophy” to Decartes’ “mechanical” one.
	- Aristotle focuses on explaining **why** things happen through causes; Descartes focuses on modeling **how** things happen.
- Francis Bacon argues that science should be about **producing effects**.
	- Bacon posits the **inductive** “scientific method”: hypothesis $\to$ predictions $\to$ testing; generalizing from samples.
	- The famous quote, “Knowledge is power,” refers to how knowledge of nature $\to$ power over nature.
- Now technology and science are intertwined.
	- The **”no miracles” argument** says that the **problem of induction** is resolved by the fact that our scientific theories work to produce effects.
	- “Why is natural philosophy able to produce techne? Because it is true. Why is natural philosophy true? Because it is able to produce techne.”
		- *Discussion.* $\text{ }$ Is this an issue?
- The relationship between **science and technology** gives a basis for the relationship between **cognitive science and artificial intelligence.**
	- Cognitive science is about reverse-engineering intelligence (natural philosophy), while AI is about engineering intelligence (techne).
	- *Discussion.* $\text{ }$ In what ways do developments in artificial intelligence help us understand human intelligence?

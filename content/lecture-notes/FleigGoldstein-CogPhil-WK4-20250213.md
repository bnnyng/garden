# Week 4, Day 2: Hereditary Pathways
---

**Presentation: [[@2020mitchell]] Ch. 5, “The Nature of Nurture”**

- Extreme childhood experiences are correlated with abnormal behavioral development. However, according to Mitchell, the robustness of behavior to slight differences in parenting suggests “family environment has little to no effect on variance in psychological traits.”
- Brain circuitry is spontaneous prior to birth, responsive to environment afterward. 
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ There are two distinct issues: what is the overall effect of nature vs. nature of a trait, and does family environment have *more* effect than others, for example broad social environment?

**Genes and behavioral variance**

- **Mendelian** genes are abstract templates for the inheritance of **traits**—organism-level characteristics—across generations; **molecular** genes **segments** of DNA that encodes a protein.
	- DNA specifies sequences of **amino acids**, which fold into proteins that **self-assemble** into larger structures.
- **Monozygotic**, or **identical** twins, demonstrate different psychological traits despite sharing DNA and family environments.
	- Behavioral geneticists classically attributed this to “non-shared environment”, e.g., subtle differences in parent treatment, different friend groups.
- Upshot: psychological differences cannot be directly attributed genes, internally driven developmental processes, and types of environmental stimuli.
- *Question.*$\ \text{}$ Is this in conflict with Buckner’s DoGMA?

**How does genetic variance bear on deep learning architectures?**

- Melchner, et. al. “Visual behavior mediated by retinal projections directed to the auditory pathway.”
	- Researchers rerouted visual input from the retina to the auditory thalamus, found that the auditory cortex developed functional visual responses.

# Week 10, Day 1: Imagination
---

**Why is imagination important?**

- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Why is imagination important? Why do we need to go from abstract ideas to particulars?
- Contra Locke, Hume’s **exemplar view** of representation holds that there are no truly abstract representations.
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ How should we define imagination, beyond the reconstruction of perceptual experience that we’ve mainly discussed?
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ C. Peacocke draws a distinction between **imagination** and **imaging**: you can imagine a cat hidden behind a suitcase without necessarily having a picture of the situation in my head; you can conceptualize an $n$-dimensional sphere for arbitrary $n > 3$, but not visualize it.

**Challenges with imagination**

- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Why can’t imagination be explained as a recombination of properties already experienced (e.g., imagining a unicorn as horse + horn)?
	- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$Why can’t recombination be viewed as “truly creative”?

**Presentation: [[@2019zerilliTransparency]], “Transparency in Algorithmic and Human Decision-Making”**

- **Explainable AI and the “inspectability of algorithms”:** 
	- **Explainable AI** is socially desirable—if we do not know how AI is making decisions, how can we trust it to make decisions?
	- A rebuttal for the project is that we are making more demands of AI than humans.
- **Practical reasons and Dennett’s intentional stance:**

| Levels      | What we consider                | Example                                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Physical    | Physics, chemistry              | Mary’s neurons fired in a certain way to lead to her behavior.        |
| Design      | Biology, engineering principles | Mary’s brain has a mechanism for producing her behavior.              |
| Intentional | Mental states                   | Mary behaved this way because of xyz facts about her life experience. |

- **Biases and transparency in algorithmic decision-making:** 
	- Human **intrinsic biases** are an adaptive advantage, but lead to unfair decision-making in many contexts. This makes AI potentially a more fair judge than humans.
	- **Extrinsic biases**, which are more readily adjusted by exposure to data, is a bigger problem for AI.

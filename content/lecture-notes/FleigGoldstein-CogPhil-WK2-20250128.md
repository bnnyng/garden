**[[Systematicity and productivity]]**

>[!example] Definition: Systematicity, productivity
>- **Systematicity** = the ability to produce or understand complex representations that share structure with a particular complex representation that has already been produced or understood.
>- **Productivity** = also known as the **principle of compositionality**; the ability to generate an infinite number of novel representations using finite resources.

- Failures of connectionist models—lack of structure, failure to generalize, and infinite novelty—can be diagnosed as issues with systematicity and productivity.

**[[The language-of-thought-hypothesis]]**

- As summarized by [[@1998cowie]], Fodor argues for a LoT with the following properties:
	- **Comparable to natural language:** Since learning is the same as testing hypotheses, all potential hypotheses must be formulable in the LoT. In particular, the LoT has to support language learning. Hence, the LoT must be as structured and rich as natural language.
	- **Unlearnable:** The hypothesis-testing model of learning is incompatible with learning a concept like “cause,” so most concepts are innate primitives in the LoT.

**[[Concept nativism, after Fodor]]**

According to J. Fodor, learning a **concept**—the building blocks of propositions—involves forming and testing a hypothesis about what that concept means. Such hypotheses are formed by compounding more primitive concepts.

Fodor’s theory of **concept nativism** resolves the issue of where the most primitive concepts—that is, the so-called **unanalyzable** concepts, which cannot be further decomposed into meaningful semantic structure—come from by proposing that primitive concepts are *automatically activated* by experience, not created from it. That is, most concepts (e.g., object permanence) are innate, and simply require a causal process to “come online.” (Hence Fodor refers to all of developmental psychology as operating a stopwatch.) 

This theory presumes that the concepts are the same for everyone, or at least isomorphic to one another.

From [[@1998cowie]]:
>What makes a concept unlearnable (hence unlearned) is features intrinsic to the concept itself—its lack of internal semantic structure, its unanalysability. … Most concepts are like CAUSE and unlike BACHELOR: they firmly resist our efforts to provide them with non-circular definitions.


From [[@1998cowie]]:
>Fodor’s nativist thinks that Mother Nature and the world do all the work in concept-acquisition, whereas the empiricist thinks that *we have to do some work as well.*

| Classical empricism                                                                                                                       | Fodorian nativism                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cause-concept relations are **rational-causal**, meaning that experiences cause concepts by “inductively supporting” semantic hypotheses. | Cause-concept relations are **brute-causal**, meaning the relation between concepts and triggering experiences are arbitrary outcomes of natural selection.                                              |
| Beyond experience, concepts must be *causally implicated* by forming and testing hypotheses about their meaning.                          | Concepts can be acquired through experience alone: “Triggering of the sensorium is, normally, causally necessary and sufficient for the availability of all concepts” (Fodor, quoted in [[@1998cowie]]). |

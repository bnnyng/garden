**Objection to science from the spatialization of time**

- Schiller’s objection: a feeling that science is conceptual violence on nature, distorting what it is to squeeze into boxes.
- **The spatialization of time:** One way to describe the founding of physics—“the founding essence”—is to add time as a dimension/parameter to the world, and physics is the project of describing patterns in four-dimensional spacetime.
	- Critique: overlooks a crucial feature of time, alluded to by the asymmetry between past and future, words like “flow” and “passage”. This makes time unlike the other spatial dimensions.
	- Quantifying time this way cuts off from science from possibility of “deep encounters with the phenomenon of the temporal,” any real insight.
	- For Heidegger, Bergson (sp?), time is essential to being.
- Albert: making 19th century romantic sentiments more concrete.

- Relations between laws, counterfactuals, and causation?
	- The idea is neutral between Humean and necessitarian views.
	- Given some laws of nature (given initial conditions, how will things be later…if deterministic), and our ordinary language notion of “cause” (see below): laws don’t give localized causes—depends on the state of the entire universe—but they seem relevant? To isolate causes, describe in terms of counterfactual dependence. To evaluate counterfactual dependence, need the physical laws and some precisification of what the event is.
	- “Cause” is puzzling: we intuitively take it be something like event $A$ causes $B$ if it is necessary that when $A$ happens then $B$ happens. But (see [[@1912russell|"On the Notion of Cause" (Russell, 1912)]]), we would basically need to know about the entire state of the universe—there are no **localized** events that satisfies this.
		- The property “I throw a rock toward the window” is not sufficient to guarantee that the window breaks.
	- Respond: Use **counterfactuals**—”if $A$ hadn’t occurred, then $B$ wouldn’t have happened.”
		- How would you distinguish between $A$ and “everything else”?
	- Lewis: What you mean by an “alternate world” is “the world that is closest to ours in which $A$ doesn’t happen.”
		- Since laws are the most compact, shared laws are very important for identifying most similar worlds.
- What is the mosaic?
	- The mosaic in its entirety (philosophically) explains why the laws are why the way they are, in the same way that a book explains a book report.
	- Humeans interpret a request for explanation as a puzzlement of how an event fits into the larger picture, which the laws describe.
- What exactly is Fodor’s anti-realist position?
	- No one wants to deny causal closure, supervenience, and that all events are physical events.
	- Fodor and Putnam try to carve out autonomy on the level of explanation.
- Issues with frequentism (i.e., view of ratio, limiting value)?
	- Sophisticated frequentist position must be enforced at the scale of the entire universe, avoiding requirements like “this coin will have to land on heads exactly half the time.”
	- A probability statement becomes a statement about the entire Humean mosaic—the mosaic is one whose fundamental laws entail such a probability.
	- Probabilities enter in dynamical equations of motion or initial condition.
- Structuralist view on law and Ramsey sentences?
	- Remember that it’s a response to positivism: all talk about theoretical entities should be reduced to talk about observations (e.g., saying there’s an electron there just means there’s a blip on the oscilloscopes).
	- Structuralism is a way of trying to rescue the essential intuition of wanting to limit yourself as much as possible to talk about observable entities.
		- Grant that electron is distinct from oscilloscope.
		- We think of our theory like this: all that our theory tells us about electrons is that they are related to oscilloscopes, protons and neutrons, in certain ways.
		- We have no grounds for believing anything about what an electron actually is—some intrinsic nature—just mathematical relations between theoretical entities and observables as well as between different theoretical entities.

# Week 12, Day 1: Social Cognition
---

**Social cognition and human feedback**

- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Is empathy required or sufficient for social cognition, or understanding other minds?
	- For example, someone (or an AI agent) may be *performatively* empathetic. Does this suffice for showing that the agent can understand other minds? Why should an ability to conform to social norms have anything to do with internal cognitive states or empathy?
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ How do we give computers goals?
	- The “swamp man” thought experiment is meant to demonstrate that evolutionary history is not needed for the existence of mental states. Upshot: “There is something weird about mental states depending on an agent’s history.”
- **Reinforcement learning with human feedback** involves the primary training of next-token prediction, then additional training for optimizing for outputs that humans like.
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{ }$ Is the existence of goals sufficient to give rise to “social cognition”?

**Cultural evolution**

- Just as **genes** are the smallest unit of natural selection, **memes** are the smallest units of cultural selection.
	- Memes do not need to contribute to the fitness of an organism, but is instead a parallel evolutionary process.
- [[Humans are the niche constructors par excellence, after Kydd|Niche construction]] is when organisms and environment mutually affect each other’s features over evolutionary time. The classic example is beavers building dams, which changes the **fitness landscape** by creating ponds they can fish in.

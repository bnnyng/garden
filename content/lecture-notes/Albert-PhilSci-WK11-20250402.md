# Week 11, Day 2: Title
---

- To **realists**, a theory that includes unobservables is suspicious. However, **positivists**, who think that the meaningful must be translated to statements about directly observable phenomena, must accept unobservables as inevitable.
- **Relativism** attempts to make a theory that does not reference absolute locations in space. 
	- A **relational** theory of time is easy to construct, but how can we make a theory of the time evolution of inter-particle distances without making reference to a location in space?
- The original debate between **relationalism** and **absolutism** was between Newton on the side of the latter, and Leibniz on the side of the former.
	- In the absolutist theory, the initial conditions must specify position and velocity. In the relationalist theory, it suffices to tell the distance between particles and their relative velocity (e.g., appealing to centrifugal force).
	- Newton argues that relationalists lack the explanatory resources to make a prediction for why the same initial conditions might lead to two different outcomes (e.g., particles starting to bob): these are indistinguishable from the relationalist perspective.

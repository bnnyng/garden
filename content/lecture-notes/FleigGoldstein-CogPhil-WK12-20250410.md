# Week 12, Day 2: Cultural Evolution
---

**Evolution and niche construction**

- **Cultural evolution** is a parallel process to Darwinian evolution: “**Differential reproduction** is all you need for evolutionary processes.”
- **Niche construction** involves a feedback process where organism and environment mutually influence each other.
	- [[Humans are the niche constructors par excellence, after Kydd]]
	- *Example.* $\quad$ Farming selects for different attributes in humans than hunting-gathering, and likely highly changed selection processes for humans. Common example is lactose tolerance gene into adulthood.
- ***Cognitive* niche construction** is based on the idea that certain cognitive strategies are more attuned to specific environments, and we construct our environments to be more conducive to those strategies.
- *Question.* $\quad$ To what extent does cultural evolution influence genetic evolution? Does the distribution of memes in a population affect the distribution of genes?

**[[Cognitive gadgets, after Heyes]]**

- Main examples of cultural evolution’s products are beliefs, tools, etc. Heyes’ theory of **cognitive gadgets** suggests that cultural evolution develops cognitive mechanisms or “programs” that can be “downloaded” into the human brain, our computing system.
	- *Example.* $\quad$ Language, counting systems, the ability to read, mind reading (i.e., social cognition, ability to reason about other people’s mental states).
	- “Encultured” minds can acquire systems set up by culture (complexes of memes) through [[Domain specificity and domain generality are graded phenomena, after Laurence and Margolis|domain-general]] cognitive mechanisms.
- Culturally transmitted cognitive gadgets select for genetically endowed cognitive traits—particularly domain-general faculties—that allow you to acquire gadgets. 
	- *Example.* $\quad$ Associative learning (both classical and reinforcement), (expert) imitation learning.

**Human flexibility**

- Primates seem to show both **matrilineal** and **patrilineal societies** (which sex remains in the group; for example, in matrilineal societies, female children remaining the group, females are all genetically related, females have tighter kinship and are therefore more dominant in the group).
- Human evolutionary trajectories has been about offloading contingencies.
	- Humans are more **neotenous** than other species, meaning they retain juvenile features, such as cognitive flexibility, for longer in development.

**Robot culture**

- *Discussion.* $\quad$ Should we design robots to install our cultural operating system? Should they share our culture, or develop their own? Could we have advanced, intelligent species without cultural evolution?

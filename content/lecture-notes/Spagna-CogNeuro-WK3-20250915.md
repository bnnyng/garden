## An overview of the nervous system

**Components of the nervous system**

- The nervous system consists of two parts: the **central nervous system (CNS)**, which has the brain and spinal cord, and the **peripheral nervous system (PNS)**, which is “way more complicated.”
	- The PNS has two components: **autonomic** and **somatic**; the autonomic NS in particular consists of **sympathetic** and **parasympathetic** components.
		- **Autonomic NS:** responsible for “automatic” action.
		- **Somatic NS:** receives sensations.
	- The CNS communicates with the rest of the body via the PNS: “No PNS, action.”

**Brain development**

![[Pasted image 20250915145132.png]]

- An embryo’s brain develops from three *bulges*, or patches of cells: the forebrain, midbrain. and hindbrain; these three patches expand and give rise to the the *entire* nervous system.
	- Forebrain → cerebral hemispheres; thalamus, hypothalamus
	- Midbrain → upper brain stem structures (e.g., tectum, tegmentum)
	- Hindbrain → cerebellum, pons, medulla

**Common features of every CNS**

![[Pasted image 20250915150300.png]]

- ⭐️ All neurons are similar in architecture—*the function of the neuron within the nervous system depends on the connections to that neuron*.
	- Evidence: wide range of **primary sensory areas** have been identified.

**The spinal cord**

![[Pasted image 20250915153612.png]]

- Information is communicated from the brain to body via the **spinal cord**.
- The spinal cord contains both gray and white matter:
	- **Gray matter:** contains cell bodies and dendrites of neurons.
	- **White matter:** consists of insulated axons, which carry information over long distances.

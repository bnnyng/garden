# Week 13, Day 2: Multiple realization arguments
---

- [[@1974fodorSpecial]] and [[@1975putnamPhilosophy]] both resist claims of reduction using the strategy of **multiple realizability**.

[[@1974fodorSpecial]]

- *Example from [[@1974fodorSpecial]].* $\quad$ There are cases of transferring money—e.g., pieces of paper changing locations in space, electrons moving in computers, signing a check—which have nothing **physical** in common with each other. 
	- That is, their **economic** significance do not share any meaningful physical properties; physics does not explain why these events have economic significance.
- Since laws of special sciences are usually not **exceptionless**, a physical translation must explain why this is the case.
- *Example.* $\quad$ It is comprehensible to say “the computer is working on a math problem” because there is a higher level of correspondence; the correspondence is not in terms of fundamental physical variables.
- Fodor argues that each science has distinct **natural kinds** (e.g., positions and velocities in physics; mental dispositions in psychology), and the natural kinds in special sciences have no correspondence to natural kinds in physics.
	- This implies that there are ways of “[[Carving nature at its joints]]” which will be extremely different between the sciences, regardless of how our knowledge of these natural kinds evolves.
- *Albert’s view.* $\quad$ These attempts to resist reductivism come from a psychological aversion to characterizing oneself as a set of physical events.

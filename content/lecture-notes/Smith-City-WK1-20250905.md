# Week 1
---

**Figure-ground representations of cities**

- Binary representation of information for where there are buildings and where this open space; most popular representation of cities.
	- *Example.*$\quad$ Map of NYC buildings; buildings are “figure,” absence of information/voids are ground.
	- *Example (most famous).*$\quad$ The Nolli Map, 1748. Note that the Vatican is in the upper left, significantly different **morphology** from rest of the city → “Vatican and Rome are two different cities.”
		- Church interiors are represented as “ground” → churches are essentially public spaces.
	- Compare: Dubai, Sacramento, New York, Paris.
- What kind of information can be derived from a figure-ground representation?
	- Proportion of figure to ground → **density** (think of downtown vs. suburbia, uncertain where the roads even are in the latter)
		- Note that density is *not* about population
	- “Good for initial information, but could be misleading” (e.g., no height information, sparse figure-ground could really be more dense).
- *Example.* $\quad$ Baychester and Co-op City (the Bronx); traditional grid vs. modernist conception.
- Figure-ground technique fell out of style with midcentury modernism (e.g., Le Corbusier’s “Plan Voisin for Paris”).

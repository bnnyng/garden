(Late arrival due to exam in preceding class.)

- **Vesalius** (1514–1564) produced a series of detailed anatomical illustrations that depicted the brain’s faculties as *not* going through the **ventricles**, which were presumably holes in the head filled with cerebrospinal fluid.
	- The **ventricular theory** held that cognition happened *in* the ventricles.
	- Note that **Copernicus’** (posthumous) introduction of the heliocentric system was in 1543, often the date given for the beginning of modern science.
- Psychological localization is distinct from neurological localization: you can maintain that, e.g., memory is a distinct faculty from imagination while accepting the notion that these faculties can be physically implemented in similar locations.
- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ Is it easier to remember whether you’ve driven behind a baby blue Ford Tacoma, or whether you’ve slow danced with John Travolta?

**Presentation: [[@1995mcclelland]], “Complementary learning systems in the hippocampus and neocortex”**

- Key questions: Why is a **hippocampal system** necessary for consolidating memories? Why does consolidation happen at different timescales? 
- Hippocampus has more immediate **plasticity**, while the neocortex stores underlying knowledge via **gradual accumulation** over multiple instances of activation.
	- McClelland et al. call this the **complementary learning systems hypothesis**.

![[Pasted image 20250311140307.png]]

- **Interleaved learning**—that is, learning that happens gradually over repeated exposures (cf. **focused learning**, which involves presentation of new information)—enables **generalization** via **similarity judgements**.
- One limitation of focused learning is **catastrophic interference**, when new information interferes with retrieval of stored memories; McClelland et al. argue that this is precisely why we have separate learning systems.
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ Learning that penguins are birds that can swim but not fly might interfere with stored information about birds and fish during focused learning.
- Thus, the hippocampal system provides a medium for initial storage of memories that avoids interference, and memories are stored gradually to limit focused learning (again avoiding interference).
	- As a metaphor, the hippocampus is like a set of flashcards that the rest of the cortex learns from during sleep, etc.
- *Discussion.* $\ \text{}$ Is this memory system compatible with Buckner’s DoGMA?
	- Upshot of the paper: Connectionist models have something to do with actual brain processes. Thus, this is a perfect example of DoGMA’s success.

# Week 2, Day 1: Brain and Nervous System
---

>[!example] Key terms
>- **Adaptation:** mechanisms that enhance physiological functions (e.g., reproduction) or benefit the overall system in some way (e.g., learning from past experiences).
>- **Emergent property:** a property of the overall system that is not a property inherent to individual units (neurons).
>- **”The first principle of cognitive neuroscience”:** “the mind is what the brain does.”
>- **Tractography:** a **connectional** research method that *traces* the connections between neurons, particularly in terms of inputs and outputs; a prominent example is **diffusion tensor imaging (DTI)**.
>- **Triangulation:** an ideal or standard for research when evidence from multiple families of cognitive neuroscience methods (termination, correlation, and manipulation) converge on the same theoretical result.

- Cognitive neuroscience is *distinct* from other disciplines in connecting biology—the organ (i.e., neurons and glial cells)—with psychology.
	- Cognitive neuroscience is about *understanding the relationship between brain and behavior*.

## Methods of cognitive neuroscience

![[Pasted image 20250908152730.png]]
- Cognitive neuroscience methods primarily vary primarily along three dimensions:
	- **Spatial resolution:** the method’s precision with respect to brain area.
	- **Temporal resolution:** the method’s precision with respect to time.
	- **Coverage:** how much of the brain that the method records.
- No methods currently maximize all three axes.

![[Pasted image 20250908152952.png]]

- A more sophisticated dimension is **penetration depth:** how *invasive* is this method? How much brain access is required?

![[Pasted image 20250908153104.png]]

- “No good cognitive neuroscience results can be obtained without **triangulating**”, i.e., finding converging evidence from multiple families of methods.
	- *Example.* $\quad$ If Broca’s area is responsible for speech, then lesions should interrupt speaking, stimulation should impact speech, etc.

## Connectional methods

![[Pasted image 20250908153356.png]]
- **Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI)** is a **tractography** method that maps input-output connections between neurons in living brains—some researchers build their entire careers off of this, remember the acronym!

## Correlational methods

![[Pasted image 20250908153700.png]]
- **Correlational methods** observe the entire brain to find the co-occurance of two phenomena: brain activity and a specific behavior.
- There are three subfamilies of correlational methods:
	- **Invasive and direct methods:** require physical access/entry to the skull. 
		- *Example.*$\quad$ **Electrophysiology** (recording electrical activity from physiological responses), e.g., Hubel and Wiesel inserting an electrode directly on the occipital lobe (visual cortex) of a cat.
		- *Example.*$\quad$ **Electroencephalography (EEG)** (less invasive), still connects electrical activity from neurons through the skull, but only using nodes placed on the scalp.
	- **Less invasive and direct:**
		- *Example.* $\quad$ **Magnetoencephalography (MEG)**, a “sister method” to EEG that measures changes in the magnetic field around the head (which result from changes in electrical activity).

## Review of previous lecture

- Two ways of classifying neurons: by **function** and by **morphology**.
	- **Functional classifications:** whether they are sensory or motor, etc.
	- **Morphological classifications:** multipolar, bipolar, unipolar, etc.

**Receptors**

![[Pasted image 20250922144915.png]]

- ⭐️ Neurotransmitters do not enter the postsynaptic neuron; they only bind to receptors on the membrane of the postsynaptic cell.

![[Pasted image 20250922145041.png]]

- Two types of receptors coexist on the postsynaptic neuron membrane, which have two different functions.
	- **Ionotropic receptors:** change their shape to allow ions to flow across the membrane.
	- **Metabotropic receptors:** cause a “chain” of metabolic reactions; no “opening” of the receptor.
- Metabotropic receptors enable channels to be open longer, even after a neurotransmitter is “spit out”

![[Pasted image 20250922145627.png]]

- Mechanisms for removal happen after the neurotransmitter is detached from the postsynaptic neuron
- SSRIs: when there is more serotonin sitting in the synaptic cleft, the postsynaptic neuron is prompted to place more receptor proteins in the membrane (but also leads to **habituation**, where more signals are required to activate the neuron)

## Neuron spiking

**Action potentials**

![[Pasted image 20250922151245.png]]

- Why do myelin help the axon potential travel faster?
	- Myelin prevent ions from entering or exiting the cell wherever the axon is insulated → action potentials only occur at the nodes of Ranvier
	- At each node, you can have mechanisms that inhibit action potential
- Question: why isn’t the entire axon myelinated?
	- Trading off speed with plasticity
	- Nodes reactivate the signal

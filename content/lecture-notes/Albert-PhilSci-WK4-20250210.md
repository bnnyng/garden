# Week 4, Day 1: Humean Supervenience, Causation
---

**Last time: [[Albert-PhilSci-WK3-20250205|Week 3, Day 2]]**

- The **governance** or **necessitarian** view of [[Laws and causation in scientific explanation|scientific laws]] takes the world to have a **hierarchical** metaphysical structure, while the **Humean** or **descriptivist** takes the world to be “flat.” 
	- Humeans believe there is no **metaphysical** distinction between *de facto* true propositions and **nomic** propositions.
	- The entire metaphysical structure is the [[Humean mosaic]].
	- Note that both are **realist** positions that simply differ in their metaphysical theory.
- Law-like propositions are distinguished by being both concise and informative; science is about finding which propositions “get to be on the T-shirt.”
	- In a **deterministic** world, such propositions are *exceptionlessly* true.
	- In an **anarchic** world, there are no such propositions and scientific enterprise is not accommodated.
	- In a **probabilistic world**, laws are statistical patterns that summarize parts of the world.
- The [[Humean mosaic]] takes the world to be the bedrock of its own metaphysical structure. It does not make sense to ask the Humean, “Why does the world have the particular patterns it does?”

**Humean explanations**

- In contrast to Nietzsche’s critique of science as explanatorily inert and useless, Humeans disavow any illusions of offering metaphysical explanations.
- A **Humean explanation** tells you how a particular phenomena fits into the general pattern of events that you are accustomed to. This assumes that when someone asks, “Why did this thing turn out this way?”, they are expressing initial surprise due to not seeing how the event “fits on the T-shirt.”

**Supervenience and epistemology**

- “Facts about $A$ **supervene** on $B$ if, in principle, you can read off all the facts about $B$ using facts about $A$.”
	- *Example.*$\ \text{}$ Classic Cartesian dualism is the view that the domain of the *mental* does not supervene on the *physical*; we cannot read off people’s mental states based on physical facts.
	- This is equivalent to saying that there could not be two possible worlds where facts about $A$ are the same, but facts about $B$ differ.
- For Humeans, laws *supervene on particulars*. This has the significant advantage of giving a clear **epistemological** account of how science can discover laws in the first place: we need only make particular observations.
- For Necessitarians, who believe that laws *fail* to supervene on the mosaic, there is no such *mechanical procedure* for reading laws from particulars.
	- *Example.*$\ \text{}$In a world with a single particle, it could be the case that the usual deterministic laws of [[Newtonian mechanics]] hold, or that there exist no forces at all. The necessitarian has no grounds for distinguishing between these [[The method of hypothesis and problems of indiscriminate confirmation|hypotheses]].

**Causation**

- Recall that the criteria for [[Deductive-nomological explanations, after Hempel]] did not require **cause** to be a part of a scientific explanation.
- According to **Russell**, causation is a misleading and potentially harmful vestige of **prescientific** ways of thinking about the world.
	- Russell argues that causal explanations in science are only specific instances of **localizing blame**, which we do in common discourse.
		- *Example.* $\ \text{}$If someone throws a rock at a window and the window breaks, we could say that “the window broke because you threw a rock at it,” or something like “the window broke because I failed to block the rock that you threw at it.”
	- Causal discourse attempts to link different parts of the world, and hence is either trivial (i.e., referring to the entire world) or gibberish.
- While Russell’s point about formal scientific theories seems indisputably true, there exists some strong intuition that certain aspects of earlier states are more **relevant** to the current one.
- In a **counterfactual analysis of causation**, one constructs an alternate initial state and determines if the results differ.
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ If we hold everything else the same and only change the fact that you didn’t throw the rock, then the window would not break, hence you caused the window to break. 
		- But this cannot be literal, since if we don’t also account for the positions of air molecules your hand would explode.
	- *Example.* $\ \text{}$ Given that in the “real” world, you threw the rock and the window broke, the relevant **counterfactual proposition** is “If you had not thrown the rock (**antecedent**), the window would not break (**consequence**).”
- **Counterfactual claims** are assertions about other worlds.

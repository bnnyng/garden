$\quad$ Hans Reichenbach distinguishes between two kinds of theoretical entities in the philosophy of science:

- **Illata:** genuine theoretical entities which have *causal efficacy* (e.g., electrons);
- **Abstracta:** “calculation-bound entities or logical constructs” (e.g., centers of gravity, the equator).

$\quad$ Characterizing theoretical entities as *abstracta* implies an [[Philosophical views of scientific theories|instrumentalist]] position regarding that theory.

---

# Selected quotations

- **Dennett (quoted in Shoemaker, 1990):** “There is always more to learn about things we are realists about: electricity or electrons, DNA molecules or chromosomes; but there really isn't anything more to learn about centers of gravity or Mendelian genes . . . They are abstracta, and what there is to learn about them is just whatever follows from their combinatorial roles in our theories” (ibid., p. 537).

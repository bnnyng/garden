According to [[@2012jackendoff]], so-called rational thought is just intuitive thought equipped with “cognitive handles,” particularly cognitive correlates of consciousness. One plausible explanation is that [[Rational thought is thought linked to the pronunciation of language, after Jackendoff]].


>[!quote] [[@2017mccarthy]]
>Problems in general are often well posed in terms of language and language remains a handy tool for explaining them. *But the actual process of thinking—in any discipline—is largely an unconscious affair.* Language can be used to sum up some point at which one has arrived—a sort of milepost—so as to gain a fresh starting point.

>[!quote] [[@2022mccarthy]]
>I don’t really like to write things down. … I think for most people to leave things unrecorded is to leave them free to look around for fresh analogies. They go about their business and come back from time to time and report to you. A written statement—or an equation—is a sort of signpost. A waystation. It tells you where you are and gives you a new place to start from.

>[!quote] Dennett on [Mindscape](https://www.preposterousuniverse.com/podcast/2020/01/06/78-daniel-dennett-on-minds-patterns-and-the-scientific-image/)
>What language permits is the development of sort of a place to stand, you know, Archimedes and his “Give me a place to stand and I'll move the world with a lever.” Language gives us places to stand in our own cognition, which permit us then to self-stimulate, to probe, to explore our own brains. And that's what creates recursion, and that's what creates the creatures of recursion, which I think is like qualia. It creates a whole menagerie of properties that are not real properties. They are properties that are the effects. Well no it's hard to say this. They are subjective in the sense that the appreciation of the property is what brings it into existence.

>[!quote] [[@2012jackendoff]]
>When a chunk of pronunciation is meaningful, we still can’t directly perceive its meaning! We’re aware of the presence of the meaning only because the pronunciation acts as a perceivable “handle” attached to it.

>[!quote] [[@2012jackendoff]]
>The “handle” of pronunciation enables you to give the thought a reference file of its own: it’s understood as a self-standing entity in the world.

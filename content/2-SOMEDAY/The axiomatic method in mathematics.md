# Overview

In its most basic definition, the **axiomatic method** in mathematics is the practice—or frame of mind, after [[@1997mazurConjecture|Mazur]]—in which the truth of a mathematical claim can be demonstrated by deducing from a set of axioms, as well as the project of laying down the rules of this deductive “language.”

In its more general use, the axiomatic frame of mind includes not only the use of axioms to categorize a particular geometry (e.g., hyperbolic geometry) or theory (e.g., set theory), but also using collections of axioms to characterize some object (e.g., a [[Algebraic groups|group]]).

>[!quote] [[@1950bourbakiArchitecture]]
>What the axiomatic method sets as its essential aim, is exactly that which logical formalism by itself can not supply, namely the profound intelligibility of mathematics.

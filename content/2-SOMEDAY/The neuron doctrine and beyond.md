# Basic premises of the neuron doctrine

- The brain consists of distinct functional units, which are neurons.
- Neurons fire all-or-nothing action potentials; action potentials have a “refractory period” that makes this activity discrete.
- Within a neuron, information is transmitted quickly via electrical signals; outside of neurons, information is transmitted more slowly via chemical signals.
- Information flows through a neuron in a single direction. 
- A neuron will always the same impact—excitatory, inhibitory, or modulatory—on other neurons ([[Order relations on sets and fields|trichotomy]]).

---
# Beyond the neuron doctrine

- Neuron doctrine says you can abstract away from most biological details—appealing because it’s like a transistor (all or nothing, build into circuits and Turing machines)
- Every aspect has a lot of exceptions
	- Excitatory or inhibitory
	- Sub-cellular computations
	- Glial cells

# Overview and basic definition

**Maximal ideals** are those that are not contained in any other proper ideals. Note that a maximal ideal is *always* a prime ideal.

>[!example] Definition: Prime, maximal ideal
>Let $R$ is a [[Algebraic rings|commutative ring with unity]] and $I$ be an [[Ideals and quotient rings|ideal]] in $R$. If $I \neq R$, then $I\subseteq R$ is:
>- (i) A **prime ideal** for all $r, s \in R$, if $rs \in I$ then either $r \in I$ or $s \in I$;
>- (ii) a **maximal ideal** if for any other ideal $J \subseteq R$ containing $I \subseteq J$, then either $J = I$ or $J = R$.

# In general commutative rings

>[!abstract] Proposition: 
>Let $R$ be a [[Algebraic rings|commutative ring with unity]] and $I$ be an [[Ideals and quotient rings|ideal]] in $R$.
>- (i) The [[Ideals and quotient rings|quotient ring]] $R / I$ is an [[Cancellation laws and integral domains|integral domain]] *if and only if* $I$ is a prime ideal.
>- (ii) $R / I$ is a [[Algebraic fields|field]] *if and only if* $I$ is a maximal ideal.
>
>As a corollary, a maximal ideal is a prime ideal.

*Proof of (i) from [[MATH-GU4042|Modern Algebra II]].*

Note that $I \neq R$ *if and only if* $R/I \neq 0$, so it suffices to show that the following conditions are equivalent:
- For all $r, s \in R$, if $rs \in I$ then either $r \in I$ or $s \in I$;
- $R / I$ has no (nonzero) divisors of $0 =  0 + I$.

But as stated in the definition of an [[Cancellation laws and integral domains|integral domain]], the second condition is equivalent to the condition that for all $r, s \in R$, if $r + I \neq 0 + I \in R / I$ and and $s + I \neq 0 + I$, then the [[Ideals and quotient rings|coset product]] 
$$ (r + I) (s + I) = rs + I \neq 0 + I \in R / I $$ as well. This means precisely that if $r \notin I$ and $s \notin I$, then $rs \notin I$, so $I$ satisfies the definition of a prime ideal. $\ \square$

*Proof of (ii) from [[MATH-GU4042|Modern Algebra II]].*

Again, $I \neq R$ *if and only if* $R / I \neq 0$, so it suffices to show that the we have $J = I$ or $J = R$ for all ideals $J$ containing $I$ *if and only if* every nonzero coset $r + I \in R/I$ has a multiplicative inverse.

- ($\implies$) Let $r + I \in R/I$ be nonzero, i.e., $r \notin I$. Now consider the  $$ J = \{ sr + t \ : \ s \in R, t \in I \} $$ is an ideal of $R$ containing $I$ and $r$. $\ \square$

>[!abstract] Corollary: A maximal ideal is a prime ideal.

*Proof.* $\ \text{ }$If $I \subseteq R$ is a maximal ideal, then $R/I$ is a field, so it is certainly an integral domain, which implies $I$ is also a prime ideal. $\ \square$

>[!abstract] Theorem ([[MATH-GU4042|Modern Algebra II]] 3.1): Equivalence of prime ideals, maximal ideals, and irreducibility in polynomial rings
>Let $I$ be an ideal in $F[x]$, where $F$ is a [[Algebraic fields|field]]. Then the following are equivalent:
>- (i) $I$ is a maximal ideal;
>- (ii) $I$ is a prime ideal and $I \neq \{ 0\}$;
>- (iii) There exists an irreducible polynomial $p$ such that $I = (p)$.

# Overview

The closest specification of an arbitrary [[Algebraic groups|group]] up to [[Group homomorphisms and isomorphisms|isomorphism]] is given by its **presentation**. 

However, two different presentations can lead to isomorphic groups regardless, and it is not possible to determine whether two groups with different presentations are isomorphic.

---
# Generating set of a group

>[!example] Definition: Generating set of a group
>If $G$ is a [[Algebraic groups|group]], a **generating set** of $G$ is a subset such that every element $g \in G$ can be expressed as a combination of *finitely* many elements of the set and their inverses under the group operation.
>
>Alternatively, if $S \leq G$ is a subset, then $\langle S \rangle$ denotes the subgroup generated by $S$, which is the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing every element of $S$. Similarly, $\langle \langle S \rangle \rangle$ is the smallest [[Normal subgroups and quotient groups|normal subgroup]] generated by elements of $S$, or the intersection of all normal subgroups of $G$ with the elements of $S$.

# Generators and relations

>[!example] Definition: Word, reduced word
>Given a [[Algebraic groups|group]]  $G$, a **word** is any product of elements of $G$ and their inverses. That is, if $S = \{ s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$ is a set of elements of $G$, a **word** or **relation** is an expression of the form 
>$$
>s_{i_1}^{e_1}s_{i_2}^{s_2}\cdots s_{i_k}^{e_k},$$ 
>where $e_i \in \mathbb Z$ for all $i$. The elements $s_j \in S$ are called **generators**, and each word in $S$ represents another element of $G$. 


>[!example] Definition: Group presentation by generators and relations
>Let $S$ be a set of **generators** and $R$ be a set of **relations**, or words, among those generators. Consider $F(S) = \mathbb Z * \cdots * \mathbb Z$, the [[Free products of groups|free group of rank n]], and suppose the $i$-th copy of $\mathbb Z$ has generator $g_i$. Then there is a function $r : R \to F(S)$ defined by
>$$
>g_{i_1}^{e_1} \cdots g_{i_k}^{e_k} \mapsto (g_{i_1}^{e_1}) \cdots (g_{i_k}^{e_k}),
>$$
>a product in $F(S)$ which may be simplified to obtain a [[Free products of groups|reduced word]]; note the similarity of $r$ to the [[Projection and inclusion maps|inclusion]] function. 
>
>The group **presentation** is “the group generated by $S$ with relations given by $R$,” written
>$$
>\langle S | R \rangle := F(S) / \langle \langle r (R) \rangle\rangle
>$$
>where $\langle \langle r (R) \rangle\rangle$ is the smallest [[Normal subgroups and quotient groups|normal subgroup]] of $F(S)$ containing $r(R)$,

# Examples

#### Free group on $n$ generators

Let $S = \{ g_1, \ldots, g_n\}$ and $R = \varnothing$. Then 
$$
\langle S | \varnothing = F(x) \cong F_n,
$$
where $F_n$ is the [[Free products of groups|free group of rank n]], otherwise known as the free group on $n$ generators with no relations. If $n=1$, then 
$$
\langle g | \varnothing \rangle = \{ g^m \ | \ m \in \mathbb Z \} \cong \mathbb Z,
$$
the unique infinite [[Cyclic groups|cyclic]] group.

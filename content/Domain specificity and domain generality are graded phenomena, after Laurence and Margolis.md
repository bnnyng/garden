![[@2023margolis#^69d373]]

[[@2023margolis]] define **domain specificity** and **domain generality** of cognitive mechanisms in virtue of the number of distinct **content domains** that they are *directed at*. This emphasizes that domain specificity is not related to the *size*, or number of entities, in the subject domain. 

Domain-general mechanisms are also **multiply directed**, meaning they do not collapse unrelated content domains into a single category, but are instead directed at each domain separately when information from the given domain is being processed:

> A domain-general concept learning mechanism would be capable of acquiring concepts in a variety of domains not in virtue of properties that all these concepts have in common as members of a single larger content domain, but rather in virtue of properties that they each have that make them members of their respective different domains.

On the other hand, a maximally domain-specific mechanism is directed at one content domain. This suggests that domain specificity and generality are *graded phenomena*: we can say one mechanism is more domain-specific (or domain-general) than another to the extent that it has a lower (or higher) degree of multi-directedness.

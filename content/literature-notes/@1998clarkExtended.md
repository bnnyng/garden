# Key terms

- **Epistemic action:** an action that “alters the world to aid and augment cognitive processes such as recognition and search”; in contrast to **pragmatic actions** that alter the world to make some physical change that is desirable for its own sake.

# Reading notes

## Extended cognition

- **Human minds depend heavily on “external supports” for problem-solving:** “Thus consider the use of pen and paper to perform long multiplication (McClelland et al. 1 Clark 1989), the use of physical re-arrangements of letter tiles to prompt word recall in Scrabble (Kirsh 1995), the use of instruments such a nautical slide rule (Hutchins 1995), and the general paraphernalia of language, books, diagrams, and culture. In all these cases the individual brain performs some operations, while others are delegated to manipulations of external media. Had our brains been different, this distribution tasks would doubtless have varied.”

>Epistemic action, we suggest, demands spread of epistemic credit. If, as we confront some task, a part of the world functions as a process which, *were it done in the head*, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is (so we claim) part of the cognitive process. (8)

## Active externalism

- **The portability criterion for cognitive processes:** “Here, we are moved by a vision of what might be called a package of resources and operations we can always bear on cognitive task, regardless of the local environment. On this view, the trouble with coupled systems is that they are too easily decoupled. The true cognitive processes are those that lie at the constant core of the s else is an add-on extra.” (10)

## From cognition to mind

>The moral is that when it comes to belief, there is nothing sacred about skull and skin. What makes some information count as a belief is the role it plays, and there is no reason why the relevant role can be played only from inside the body. (14)

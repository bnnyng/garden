# Key terms

>[!example] Key terms in [[@1951quineTwo]]
>- **Analytic truths:** truths that are grounded in meanings *independently* of fact.
>- **Synthetic truths:** truths that are grounded in some fact of the matter about the world, e.g., empirical observation.
>- **Extension:** the class (possibly empty) of all entities for which a general term, or **predicate**, is true.
>- **Reductionism:** “the belief that each meaningful statement is equivalent to some logical construct upon terms which refer to immediate experience.”

# Reading notes

## Empiricism without the dogmas

> As an [[Nativist and empiricist positions in cognitive science|empiricist]] I continue to think of the conceptual scheme of science as a tool, ultimately, for predicting future experience in the light of past experience. Physical objects are conceptually imported into the situation as convenient intermediaries—not by definition in terms of experience, but simply as [[Irreducibility|irreducible]] posits comparable, epistemologically, to the gods of Homer. (41)

- **Physical objects and entities posited by religion have the same epistemological footing:** “In point of epistemological footing the physical objects and the gods \[of Homer] differ only in degree and not in kind. Both sorts of entities enter our conception only as cultural posits. The myth of physical objects is epistemologically superior to most in that it has proved more efficacious than other myths as a device for working a manageable structure into the flux of experience” (41).
	- **(Example) Analogy to the rational numbers and extensions of the number system:** “The physical objects are no more [[Irreducibility|reducible]] to experience than the irrational numbers are to rational numbers, but their incorporation into the theory enables us to get more easily from one statement about experience to another.
	- “\[Forces and mathematics] are on the same footing with physical objects and gods, neither better nor worse except for differences in the *degree* to which they expedite our dealings with sense experiences” (42).
- **Positing of physical objects co-evolved with the development of language for reference:** “The positing of physical objects is far more archaic, indeed being coeval, I expect, with language itself. For language is social and so depends for its development on intersubjective reference.”
	- See also: [[@2017mccarthy|"The Kekulé Problem" (McCarthy, 2017)]]

> Science is a continuation of common sense, and it continues the common-sense expedient of swelling ontology to simplify theory.

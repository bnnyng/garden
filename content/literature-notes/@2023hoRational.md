# Overview

#### Summary

$\quad$ This paper proposes that conceptual inflexibility during planning arises due to two cognitive costs: the effort required to represent details of a problem and the effort required to switch to a new representation of a problem. The paper extends [[(Model) Value-guided task construals, after Ho et al. (2022)|value-guided task construals]] by formalizing **construal strategies** for selecting a *family* of construals to consider.

#### Key terms

- **Functional fixedness:** the tendency to overlook relevant but unfamiliar ways of using an object to solve a problem (i.e., , even while actively searching for a solution.
- **Conceptual rigidity:** when a problem-solver limits which causal [[Affordances|affordances]] of a problem they are representing while planning; contrasted with **procedural rigidity**, where problem-solvers limit actions to precompiled routines or a prior plan.
- **Task set:** the set of features of a stimulus to attend to in order to guide actions.

# Background

> ***What are the main theories in the literature? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- The phenomenon of **functional fixedness** is not accounted for in classical theories of planning.
	- **Heuristic search** models (e.g., [[@1976newellComputer]]) assume that the problem solver has performs computations over a *fixed* (implicitly, fully specified) task representation.
	- Thus, it is assumed that successful planning depends on choosing the *right* representation—does not focus on flexibility with new problems.

# Argument and hypotheses

>***Does the article advance a new theoretical argument? If yes, what is it?***

- People actively engaged in goal-directed planning can fail to use the *best* representation for a problem due to **complexity costs** and **switch costs**.
	- **Complexity costs** correspond to the amount of detail being represented, and are a function about the cognitive resources available to solve the *immediate* problem at hand.
	- **Switch costs** correspond to the effort of changing the family of representations *across* problems.

>***What are the hypotheses to be tested?***

- **”Functional fixedness reflects the avoidance of complexity and switching costs during planning.” (10)**
	- **Prediction:** This implies that since the *blocks-only* construal strategy has a lower complexity than the *blocks-and-notches* strategy, people will tend to prefer the former when possible (see methods).
	- **Prediction:** When solving mazes in sequence, the cost of switching representational strategies will bias participants towards re-using the previous strategy, even when it leads to a suboptimal path.

# Research design

>***What is the methodological approach?***

- **Experimental paradigm:** Participants consider 2D mazes which include walls with *blocks* that prevent movement and *notches* that can be traversed. 
	- Mazes can be represented in terms of block only, or both blocks and notches; the two families of representations induce two distinct **construal strategies** that can be applied to a given problem.

# Conclusion

>***What is the overall conclusion?***

- People simplify problems and persist in their construal strategies even when a more attending to more details would lead to a better solution.
	- For example, people adopt a blocks-only construal strategy if navigating through notches was optional for solving the problem, and continue to ignore notches. 
- The [[Computational, algorithmic, and implementation level analyses of information-processing systems, after Marr (1982)|computational level]] principles underlying functional fixedness—avoidance of complexity and switching costs—are confirmed by modeling patterns of optimal behavior, suboptimal behavior, and reaction times in the [[(Model) Value-guided task construals, after Ho et al. (2022)|value-guided task construal]] framework.

>***How did this study advance the literature? What are the main contributions?***

- Integrates ideas from **task switching** and **planning** in a single formal framework. 
- **Experimental paradigm:** The paper introduces a novel paradigm where participants navigated mazes that could be represented *simply* as blocks or more *complexly* as blocks and notches.
	- This paradigm evaluates functional fixedness, like classic studies, and affords trial-by-trial analysis, as in contemporary decision-making research.
	- The *trial-based* design eliminates the effects of how problems are initially presented to participants.
- **New theoretical perspective:** The results demonstrate the phenomenon of **conceptual rigidity**, which extends previous studies of **procedural rigidity**.

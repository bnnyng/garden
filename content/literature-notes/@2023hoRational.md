# Overview

#### Summary

$\quad$ This paper proposes that conceptual inflexibility during planning arises due to two cognitive costs: the effort required to represent details of a problem and the effort required to switch to a new representation of a problem.

#### Key terms

- **Functional fixedness:** the tendency to overlook relevant but unfamiliar ways of using an object to solve a problem (i.e., , even while actively searching for a solution.

# Background

> ***What are the main theories in the literature? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- The phenomenon of **functional fixedness** is not accounted for in classical theories of planning.
	- **Heuristic search** models (e.g., [[@1976newellComputer]]) assume that the problem solver has performs computations over a *fixed* (implicitly, fully specified) task representation.
	- Thus, it is assumed that successful planning depends on choosing the *right* representation—does not focus on flexibility with new problems.

# Argument and hypotheses

>***Does the article advance a new theoretical argument? If yes, what is it?***

- People actively engaged in goal-directed planning can fail to use the *best* representation for a problem due to **complexity costs** and **switch costs**.
	- **Complexity costs** correspond to the amount of detail being represented, and are a function about the cognitive resources available to solve the *immediate* problem at hand.
	- **Switch costs** correspond to the effort of changing the family of representations *across* problems.

# Research design

>***What is the methodological approach?***

- Researchers designed the **Moral Machine** game, in which participants are shown unavoidable accident scenarios with two possible outcomes, depending on whether the vehicle *swerves* or *stays on course* (similar to the classic [[Trolley problems|trolley problem]]).

>***What data is used?***

- Accident scenarios vary over nine factors: sparing humans vs. pets, staying on course vs. swerving, sparing passengers vs. pedestrians, saving more vs. fewer lives, sparing men vs. women, sparing the young vs. the elderly, sparing pedestrians who cross legally vs. jaywalking, sparing the fit vs. the less fit, sparing those with higher vs. lower social status.
- Demographic data was collected on age, education, gender, income, and political and religious views.

# Results

>***What are the key findings?***

- **Three key global preferences:** strong global preferences for sparing humans over animals, sparing more lives, and sparing young lives.
- **No opposing effects in individual variations:** individual (demographic) variations had no significant effect on any of the nine features studied, and none split the population into opposing directions of effect.
- **Three distinct cultural clusters:** hierarchical clustering of scores revealed three distinct clusters: Western, Eastern, and Southern.

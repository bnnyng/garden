# Results

>***What are the key findings?***

***In vitro research*: Simulating the discovery of genetic control**

- **Study 1 – Discovering inhibitors:** Subjects set a goal of finding evidence consistent with their original hypothesis (i.e., that genes are activators that switch on enzyme production), and when all experimental evidence was inconsistent with the activation hypothesis, the subjects who maintained the original goal were “blocked” from setting new goals (i.e., discovering the *cause* of unexpected findings).
- **Study 2 – Discovering both activators and inhibitors:** After first achieving their initial goal (i.e., discovering activation), twice as many subjects were able to set a new goal of accounting for unexpected findings and then generate an inhibitory hypothesis.

***In vivo research*: Mechanisms underlying conceptual change and insight

- **Inconsistent evidence leads to discarding hypotheses in group settings:** 
	- Inconsistent evidence tended to lead to *minimal* conceptual change—specific hypothesis features were adjusted while the overall type remained the same.
	- When evidence was inconsistent with an entire *type* of hypothesis, individual scientists tended to attribute inconsistency to errors, while groups tended to focus on the inconsistency and induce conceptual change in the individual.

# Conclusion

>***What is the overall conclusion?***

***In vitro research*: Simulating the discovery of genetic control**

- **Successful discovery requires switching goals to investigate unexpected discoveries:** “The goal that subjects set was pivotal to making a discovery. When subjects maintained their initial goal they did not make a discovery. When subjects changed their goal to one of exploring the cause of unexpected and/or inconsistent findings, they then made the discovery.”
	- #concept-question  In practice, do we find such “discoveries” justified?
	- 💬 Result seems to be the phenomenon modeled in [[@2023hoRational]]: people reduce cognitive costs by maintaining the same goal, even when switching goals is more optimal.

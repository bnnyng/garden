# Summary

> [!Abstract]
>
> This article presents the case for an approach to consciousness that I call illusionism. This is the view that phenomenal consciousness, as usually conceived, is illusory. According to illusionists, our sense that it is like something to undergo conscious experiences is due to the fact
that we systematically misrepresent them (or, on some versions, their objects) as having phenomenal properties. Thus, the task for a theory of consciousness is to explain our illusory representations of phenomenality, not phenomenality itself, and the hard problem is replaced by the illusion
problem. Although it has had powerful defenders, illusionism remains a minority position, and it is often dismissed as failing to take consciousness seriously. This article seeks to rebut this accusation. It defines the illusionist programme, outlines its attractions, and defends it against
some common objections. It concludes that illusionism is a coherent and attractive approach, which deserves serious consideration.
>.

# Key terms

>[!example] Key terms from [[@2016frankish]]
>- **Radical realism** = the view that phenomenal consciousness is real and inexplicable without major theoretical revisions to current science.
>- **Conservative realism** = the view that phenomenal consciousness is real and can be explained in physical terms (e.g., using current cognitive science).
>- **The illusion problem** = the problem of explaining how phenomenality arises and why it is so powerful, if phenomenal consciousness is indeed an illusion.
>- **Introspective subjectivity** = an introspective awareness of experiences generated by representational mechanisms; this is the formulation of “what it is like” that illusionists can accept.
>- **Intrinsic subjectivity** = “a subjective dimension that is not the product of introspective mechanisms but arises simply from our being the things we are.”
>

# Reading notes

## Introducing illusionism

- **Illusionism denies that conscious experiences have phenomenal properties:** “Illusionism … allows that we are introspectively aware of our sensory states but argue that this awareness is partial and distorted, leading us to misrepresent the states as having phenomenal properties.”
- **Some analogies for illusionism:** “Thee analogies all illustrate the basic illusionist claim that introspection delivers a distorted, partial view of our experiences, misrepresenting complex physical features as simple phenomenal ones.”
	- Graphical interfaces (Dennett); “stabilities in our reactions to the world induce us to project corresponding properties onto the world” (Rey); secondary qualities like colors (Pereboom); visual illusions.
- **Illusionists interpret “what it is like” as *introspective* subjectivity, rather than *intrinsic* subjectivity:** “Illusionists will deny the equivalence between having an inner life and having phenomenal consciousness. Having the kind of inner life we have … consists in having a form of introspective self-awareness that creates the illusion of a rich phenomenology.”
	- “One’s experiences are *like something* if one is aware of them in a functional sense, courtesy of introspective representational mechanisms.”

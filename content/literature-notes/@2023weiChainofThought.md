# Summary

>[!example] Key terms
>- **Chain of thought:** a series of intermediate reasoning steps generated by a large language model.
>- **Chain-of-thought prompting:** an engineering technique of providing a few chain-of-thought exemplars during prompting of an LLM.


---

# Topic, thesis

> ***Briefly, why is the research question important to answer?***

- Chain of thought may be applicable to any task that humans can use natural language to perform, including math word problems, commonsense reasoning, and general symbol manipulation.
- Chain of thought gives a method for interpreting the “behavior” of a model. 

---
# Background

> ***What are the main theories in the literature? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- Human reasoning seems to involve decomposing a problem into at **chain-of-thought** involving multiple subproblems, which are solved in sequence. 

>***What is the empirical evidence? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- Arithmetic reasoning can be improved by generating natural language rationales—intermediate steps—that lead to the final answer.
- Models are capable of **in-context few-shot** learning via **prompting**: instead of training and finetuning a model for a particular task, a model can learn via a few input-output exemplars provided in a prompt (Brown et al., 2020).
	- However, the traditional method does not work well on tasks that require **reasoning**.

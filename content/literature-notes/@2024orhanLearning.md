# Topic, thesis

> ***Briefly, why is the research question important to answer?***

- **Research question:** Is it possible to learn “powerful internal models” of the world, as human children do, without strong domain-specific inductive biases?
- This question relates to the [[Nativist and empiricist positions in cognitive science|nativism-empiricism]] debate.

# Argument and hypotheses

>***Does the article advance a new theoretical argument? If yes, what is it?***

- Investigates the [[Nativist and empiricist positions in cognitive science|nativism-empiricism]] debate using a “modern lens”: highly generic deep neural networks.

>***What are the hypotheses to be tested?***

- Can neural networks learn from a “representative sample of a child’s egocentric visual experience”? More specifically, what kinds of perceptual capabilities might be learnable from a child’s visual experience without inductive biases?

---
# Research design

>***What is the methodological approach?***

- **Self-supervised learning** algorithms are trained a large-scale, longitudinal developmentally realistic dataset of headcam videos. Note that each model is trained on data from a single child, “simulating the child’s **learning problem** as closely as possible.”

>***What data is used?***

- **SAYCam:** headcam videos recorded over 26 months of development across three children—472 hours of video total.
	- Continuous headcam recordings are taken for 1–2 hours each week.
	- Videos are sampled at 5 frames per second, leading to 9 million frames across three children.

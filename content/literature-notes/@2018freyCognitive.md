# Background

> ***What are the main theories in the literature? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- Formal solutions to games suggest forms of reasoning that human players might actually employ. For example, the model of unbounded iterated reasoning that formally solves the **Beauty Contest** ([[@2004camererCognitive]]) suggests that humans might perform the same iterations mentally. 
- Formal solutions do not predict any similarities in reasoning between the Beauty Contest, Mod Game, and Runway Game; of the latter two, the formal solution to the Mod Game predicts random behavior, while there is no solution to the Runway Game and hence “not a game-theoretic prescription for the type of reasoning that a player should employ.”

>***What is the empirical evidence? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- In games that elicit strategic iterated reasoning, players usually only perform 0–3 iterations; it is unclear whether this is due to individual cognitive limits or limits expected of others.

# Argument and hypotheses

>***Does the article advance a new theoretical argument? If yes, what is it?***

- “Hopping” behavior in players’ choices during a repeated game (i.e., changing their choice by more than 1 between rounds, even when rewards are given for differences of 1 from choices of other players) is best explained by **iterated reasoning**, even if an iterated model is not a formal solution to a particular game.
- In repeated games, players’ expressed *depths* of iterated reasoning are a [[Conventions are stable but arbitrary solutions to repeated coordination problems, after Lewis (1969)|social norm]] that emerges as a result of “group mates’ previous patterns of mutual adaptation to each other.”

>***What are the hypotheses to be tested?***

- Predictions made by the **norm-based theory of reasoning depth**:
	- Exhibited depths of reasoning (operationally defined as median “hop” size between choices across consecutive rounds) should vary greatly across groups but remain similar within groups;
	- An individual’s depth of reasoning should predict the median depth of the rest of the group;
	- Variations in members’ reasoning depths should decrease with time;
	- Patterns should disappear in randomly reshuffled groups.

# Research design

>***What is the methodological approach?***

**Economic [[Games of strategy|games]]**

- **$p$-beauty contest:** Players receive a reward for guessing the number between 0 and 100 that is $p$ (typically $p = 2/3$) of the group’s mean. The unique *pure-strategy Nash equilibrium* choice is $0$.
- **Mod Game:** A generalized form of rock-paper-scissors where players choose an integer between 1 and $m$. Choice 1 beats choice $m$, while all other choices $n$ beat the choice $n-1$. Players are rewarded for beating another player.
- **Runaway Game:** Like the Mod Game, players are rewarded for choosing a number exactly 1 greater than another’s. However, the choice space is the real number line.

**Sophistication and flocking**

- A player’s **rate** in round $t$ is defined as the difference between current and previous choices $x’t = x_t - x_{t-1}$. Rate corresponds to **sophistication**: rate greater than 1 is assumed to reflect an **iterated best-response** that anticipates the iterated best-responses of others.
- **Choice distance** and **rate distance** are the mean or median difference between group members’ choices and rates within a round.

# Background

> ***What are the main theories in the literature? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- Language is a “tool to organize the microstructure of cognition and action during interaction.” By providing a shared reference scheme for users to orient themselves to objects in the world, language enables a group of individuals in a single task environment to function as a single cognitive system. 

>***What is the empirical evidence? Is there anything missing or problematic?***

- Previous experiments with the **tanagram task** (defined below) show that as the task is repeated with the same set of abstract shape stimuli, participants “take less time to solve the task, require fewer words to do so, and end up with a jointly constructed scheme of shorthand descriptions for the shapes (Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs, 1986; see Clark, 1996, Chapter 3, for a detailed review).”

# Argument and hypotheses

>***Does the article advance a new theoretical argument? If yes, what is it?***

- Perceptual-motor coupling in a pair of participants is functionally analogous to a tanagram identification “device” found in individuals, indicating that “two-person systems exhibit the same loose-coupling under task constraints that a single cognitive processor exhibits, further demonstrating that *pairs of people or beyond may serve as coherent units of analysis themselves* (Tollefsen, 2002, 2006).”

# Research design

>***What is the methodological approach?***

- **Tanagram task:** two participants are shown the same set of six abstract shapes in different orders. One participant, the **matcher**, must arrange their shapes to match the order of the other, the **director**, based on the director’s verbal descriptions.

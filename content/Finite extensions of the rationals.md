# Instructive examples for Galois theory

#### $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2)$

```
\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2)
```

- [[Finite extensions of the rationals]]: This extension satisfies the property of being generated by the roots a polynomial over $\mathbb Q$, since $$ \mathbb Q(\sqrt 2) = \mathbb Q (\sqrt 2, -\sqrt 2), $$ where $\pm \sqrt 2$ are all the roots of $x^2 - 2$ lying in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2)$.
- [[Automorphisms and polynomial roots]]: $\text{Gal}( \mathbb Q(\sqrt 2) / \mathbb Q)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_2$, which is now viewed as the set of permutations of the two-element set $\{ \sqrt 2, - \sqrt 2 \}$.

#### $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3)$

- [[Finite extensions of the rationals]]: This extension satisfies the property of being generated by the roots a polynomial over $\mathbb Q$, since $$ \mathbb Q(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3) = \mathbb Q (\sqrt 2, -\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3, -\sqrt 3), $$ where $\pm \sqrt 2, \pm \sqrt 3$ are all the roots of $(x^2 - 2) (x^2 - 3)$ lying in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3)$. ***This example shows that the polynomial with roots generating the extension does not have to be irreducible.***

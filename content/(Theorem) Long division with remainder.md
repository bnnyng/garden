# Statement and proof

>[!abstract] Proposition: Long division with remainder
>Let $F$ be a [[Algebraic fields|field]] and $f, g \in F[x]$ be [[Polynomial rings|polynomials]] with $f \neq 0$. Then there exist *unique* polynomials $q, r \in F [x]$, with either $r = 0$ or $\deg r < \deg f$, such that $$ g = fq + r. $$

*Proof from [[MATH-GU4042|Modern Algebra II]].*

**Existence, case 1:** If $g = 0$, this is clearly true by taking $q = r = 0$.

**Existence, case 2:** Argue by [[(Proof pattern) Mathematical induction|induction]] on $\deg g$.
- **Base case:** If $\deg g = \deg f = 0$, then the polynomial $f$ is a nonzero constant $f = c \in F^* = F \backslash \{0\}$. The $g = c(c^{-1}g) + 0$, so we can take $q = c^{-1}g$ and $r =0$.
- If $\deg g < \deg f$, then we can take $q = 0$ and $r = g$. #concept-question  why?
- **Inductive step:** For a fixed $f$, suppose the existence of $q, r$ have been proved for polynomials of degree $<n$, and suppose $n = \deg g > \deg f$. Explicitly, write $$ f = \sum_{i=0}^d a_ix^i \ \text{ with }\ a_d \neq 0, \quad \quad g = \sum_{j=0}^n b_jx^j. $$ Then we can construct a polynomial of degree at most $n-1$ (or $0$) by the following: $$ g - b_na^{-1}_dx^{n-d}f.$$ By the inductive hypothesis, there exist polynomials $q’, r \in F[x]$ with either $r = 0$ or $\deg r < \deg f$ such that the previous expression is equal to $fq’ + r$. Thus, we can take the same $r$ and set $q = b_na^{-1}_dx^{n-d} + q’$ to complete the existence proof.

**Uniqueness:** Suppose $$ g = fq + r = fq' + r' $$ for some $r, r,’ q, q’ \in F[x]$ satisfying the previous. Then $$ (q - q')f = r' - r, $$ so either $q - q’ = 0$ and we are done, or, since $f \neq 0$, we have $$ \deg ((q - q')f) = \deg (q - q') + deg f \geq deg f $$ and $r’ - r \neq 0$. But then $$ \deg (r' - r) \geq \max \{ \deg r, \deg r' \} \leq \deg f, $$ a contradiction, so it must be the case that $q - q’ = r’ - r = 0$, which proves uniqueness. $\ \square$

Another way to state the theorem is by the following.

>[!abstract] Theorem: Long division with remainder (more obscure)
>Let $(f)$ be the [[Ideals and quotient rings|principal ideal]] generated by a *nonzero* [[Polynomial rings|polynomial]] $f \in F[x]$. Then every [[Algebraic groups|coset]] $g + (f)$ has a *unique* representative $r$ such that either $r = 0$ or $\deg r < \deg f$.
